# Singapore Private Banking — Industry Landscape

> **Day 5 of the 7-day study path.** Interviewers use industry-landscape questions to test whether you *chose* this bank deliberately or are scatter-shooting applications. Know the major players, the business model, the segment structures, and the trends. Don't memorise AUM numbers — they move and every bank rounds up — but know the strategic positioning of each player relative to the others.

**Post-1MDB and post-CS context:**

- **BSI and Falcon** — licence-revoked in 2016 after 1MDB enforcement; both effectively exited SG.
- **Credit Suisse** — absorbed into UBS in March 2023; combined entity dominates APAC PB by AUM.
- **Mergers and tier-consolidation are ongoing** — expect another wave of mid-tier consolidation through the late 2020s as scale economics bite.

## Client segmentation

The industry segments clients by wealth tier. Each bank has its own exact cutoffs; the below is the standard industry shape.

| Tier | AUM range | Bank model | Service level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Affluent / Emerging Wealth** | US$250k–1M | Mass affluent / "premier" tier — retail-plus, digital-heavy | Junior RM / digital platform; standardised products |
| **High Net Worth (HNW)** | US$1M–30M | Core PB book | Dedicated RM, basic advisory, product shelf access |
| **Ultra-High Net Worth (UHNW)** | US$30M+ | Senior PB | Senior RM, IC coverage, alternatives and PE access, bespoke structuring |
| **Family Office (FO)** | US$100M+ | Institutional-adjacent treatment | Multi-RM team, specialist IC, bespoke platform / SPV structures, direct PE co-invest |
| **Single Family Office (SFO)** | Single family, US$100M+ | Client runs their own investment team; bank is an execution / advisory counterparty | Institutional servicing model |
| **Multi-Family Office (MFO)** | Several families under one umbrella | Client's own office manages relationships; bank services | Institutional servicing model |

**Singapore-specific family-office structures:**

- **VCC (Variable Capital Company)** — SG corporate structure for fund / wealth vehicles introduced 2020. Tax-efficient for family offices and fund managers.
- **MAS Section 13O / 13U tax incentive schemes** — widely used by family offices for fund vehicles. Requires AUM thresholds (S$10M for 13O, S$50M for 13U), staff employment, and minimum local spend; in exchange, certain tax exemptions on designated investments.
- **Wealth-management services** — SG has become a regional hub for FO establishment, driven by tax incentives, political stability, stable currency, rule of law, and the 13O/13U framework.

## Revenue model

PB revenue comes from four main lines. The mix varies by client profile and mandate type.

| Revenue line | Typical size | Driver |
|---|---|---|
| **Transaction commission** | 30–45% of revenue | Trades on advisory mandates — funds, bonds, equities, structured products |
| **Management fee (% AUM)** | 20–40% | DPM mandates (flat %), advisory retainers, fund trailer fees |
| **Net Interest Margin (NIM)** on Lombard / deposits | 15–30% | Lombard loans (see `products/leverage.md`), deposit spread |
| **Structured-product issuance margin** | 5–15% | Initial margin on issuing structured notes (typical 1–3% of notional) |

**Return on Assets (ROA) benchmark:** industry-typical 60–90 bps p.a. on AUM. High-touch UHNW books closer to 50 bps. Transaction-heavy advisory with retrocessions can reach 100+ bps. DPM-only mandates around 75 bps. Each bank reports its ROA per segment; tracking the trend across years signals strategic direction — rising ROA often means trade-heavier, falling ROA means DPM-heavier.

## Career ladder

The titles vary by bank; the progression is roughly common.

| Title (descending) | Typical tenure | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **MD / Managing Director** | 15+ years | Senior RM with large book, or business-line leadership |
| **ED / Executive Director** | 10+ years | Senior RM, big book, often team leader |
| **D / Director** | 7+ years | RM with substantial book (US$400M+), or IC head of coverage |
| **AD / Associate Director / VP (VP in some banks)** | 5+ years | Full RM / senior IC role |
| **AVP / Associate Vice President / Sr Associate** | 3+ years | RM with building book, or IC in coverage role |
| **Associate / Analyst / ARM (Associate RM)** | 0–3 years | ARM support, analyst for an RM team; transition to full RM typically at year 2–3 |

**The ARM → RM promotion bar** is a frequent interview topic for early-career candidates. Varies by bank, but typically requires:

- Demonstrated ability to originate client relationships or inherit / grow an existing book.
- Passed CACS and any required CFA / CAIA certifications.
- Track record of commercial contribution — AUM growth, product revenue.
- Senior RM and team leadership endorsement.

**Compensation ranges (SG, 2024–2025, illustrative):**

| Level | Base | Variable (target) | Total target |
|---|---|---|---|
| ARM / Analyst | SGD 80–130k | 20–40% | SGD 100–180k |
| Associate / AVP | SGD 130–200k | 40–80% | SGD 180–360k |
| VP / AD | SGD 200–320k | 60–150% | SGD 320–800k |
| D | SGD 320–500k | 80–200% | SGD 600–1.5M |
| ED | SGD 500–800k | 100–300% | SGD 1M–3M |
| MD | SGD 800k+ | Significant revenue share | USD 2M–10M+ for top producers |

ICs typically earn 70–90% of equivalent-level RMs at the senior grades; less revenue-exposed but more stable. Variable compensation structure: formula-based revenue share up to ~30–50% of revenue for senior RMs at top-paying firms; discretionary component; deferred compensation / stock-vesting plans for 2–4 years.

## Industry trends (2024–2026)

**1. Digitalisation and AI.** Every PB has a digital platform — some (DBS IWM, HSBC HQ) have invested heavily; others are playing catch-up. Generative AI is entering client reporting, research, and RM assistance. Impact on RM productivity vs RM headcount is the open strategic question.

**2. Asian UHNW growth.** APAC HNW / UHNW asset pool is projected to grow faster than Europe / North America through 2030. SG specifically benefits from relative political stability, regulatory quality, and the tax-incentive framework for FO relocation from HK.

**3. Family-office influx.** Post-2020, substantial FO relocation from HK, China, and other regions to SG. The MAS 13O / 13U regime and VCC structure have accelerated this. Some regulatory tightening in late 2022–2023 as SG managed quality-of-FO inflows.

**4. Regulatory tightening post-1MDB, post-CS AT1.** Tighter SOW corroboration, stricter cross-border marketing enforcement, real-time transaction monitoring, COSMIC platform (April 2024). Raised the operational cost of doing PB in SG, but the quality of the business has improved.

**5. ESG / sustainable investing.** Every CIO house view now has a sustainable-investing pillar; every product shelf has ESG-wrapped variants. Client adoption in Asia historically lagged Europe but is growing. Watch for any ESG backlash in the US spilling into Asia.

**6. Consolidation wave.** The CS-UBS integration is the biggest; further tier-2 consolidation is expected as regulatory and platform costs compress margins for mid-size players.

**7. Talent economics.** Hiring war continues for senior RMs with transferable books. Post-CS dislocation created significant talent movement in 2023–2024; integration continues through 2026. Taiwanese, Thai, and Indonesian-focused RM talent is particularly in demand given SG's positioning for those books.

**8. Alternative access democratisation.** Lower-minimum feeder funds, 40-Act liquid-alt mutual funds, thematic structured-product wrappers — lowering the ticket for alternatives access is a 3-5 year trend; quality of exposure is a separate question.
