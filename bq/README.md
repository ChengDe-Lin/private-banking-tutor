# Behavioural Question (BQ) Story Bank

> **Day 6 of the 7-day study path.** Behavioural questions in PB interviews test six core competencies: Relationship & Trust, Commercial Judgement, Integrity / Ethics, Resilience & Composure, Cross-Cultural Sensitivity, Collaboration, Regulatory Awareness. Every BQ answer should resolve to one of these. This folder contains **10 generic STAR-format templates** — the user must personalise each one with real career specifics before walking into the interview.

## How to use this bank

1. **Read each story's structure** first — the skeleton is what works; the generic filler is placeholder.
2. **Replace every `[X]` and bracketed placeholder with real specifics from your career** — numbers, dates, names (appropriately redacted), stakes, outcomes. A generic story sounds generic; a specific story sounds credible.
3. **Practice each out loud, aiming for 2–3 minutes per story** — this is STAR-length, not PAR (Problem-Action-Result) shortcut and not essay-length sprawl.
4. **Drill the "What this demonstrates"** — the one-line competency summary. Interviewers listen for this; it's the payload.
5. **Memorise the order of 5 strongest stories** — when asked "tell me about a time you…" you want rapid recall.
6. **Map to role** — see `../roles.md`. RM candidates emphasise commercial / relationship / acquisition stories; IC candidates emphasise judgement / collaboration / specialist stories.

## STAR template

Every story follows this exact skeleton:

```markdown
# <Category>: <one-line title>

## Situation
One paragraph setting the context. Client type, stakes, timing. Specific enough to be real.

## Task
What was the challenge or decision I needed to resolve? One or two sentences.

## Action
The specific, measurable steps I took. Use "I" not "we" where you personally acted.
Numbered sub-bullets help. No "I communicated effectively" — say what exactly you did.

## Result
What happened. Include a number — AUM, retention %, revenue, client count.
If templated, use `US$[X]M` placeholders.

## What this demonstrates
One line mapping to PB competency — Relationship, Commercial, Judgement, etc.
Short. Load-bearing.
```

## Common anti-patterns to avoid

- **Vague Action** — "I communicated with the client" means nothing. Say what you said, when, and how.
- **No number in Result** — "the relationship improved" is a feeling; "we added US$8M AUM over 6 months" is a story.
- **Hero narratives** — "I singlehandedly" sounds insecure; credit your team.
- **Not-my-fault framing** — if the Situation is "the client was unreasonable," you're already lost.
- **Missing competency tie-back** — "What this demonstrates" isn't optional; it's how interviewers remember.
- **Stories that are all outcome, no tension** — if nothing was at stake, there's no story.
- **Over-polished / over-rehearsed delivery** — sounds scripted. Aim for *prepared* not *performed*.

## Prioritisation under time pressure

If you only have time to master 5 stories before the interview, prioritise by role:

**RM / Senior RM track:** 01, 03, 05, 06, 08 (or 04, 09) — heavy on relationship and commercial.

**IC / Investment Counselor track:** 03, 07, 02, 10, 04 — heavy on judgement and specialist-collaboration.

**Both roles need one integrity / AML story** (02) — non-optional. Interviewers will ask.
