# Resilience & Composure: Calmed a panicking client during a sharp sell-off

## Situation

During `[describe a specific event — e.g. March 2020 COVID sell-off, October 2022 UK-gilt dislocation, March 2023 SVB / CS AT1 episode]`, one of my clients — `[describe: e.g. a 55-year-old HNW client, US$X AUM, Balanced mandate, historically calm]` — called me in the morning after the portfolio had dropped `[Y%]` over the prior week and asked me to "sell everything and go to cash." He was uncharacteristically emotional; his wife was reading news headlines aloud; he mentioned they had considered moving `[US$ZM]` to another institution.

## Task

Prevent an emotional decision that would crystallise losses at the bottom and derail the long-term plan we'd agreed on, while genuinely hearing his concerns and not dismissing the fear.

## Action

1. **Requested a same-day face-to-face or video call, not a phone decision.** Emotional decisions don't get made better on a phone. He agreed to a 1pm video call.
2. **Listened first, pitched nothing.** Let him and his wife walk through their fears fully. Didn't interrupt. Didn't correct market commentary even when factually wrong. Took ~15 minutes just listening.
3. **Re-grounded in the plan we'd agreed to 18 months earlier.** Pulled up the Investment Policy Statement we'd jointly drafted, which specifically anticipated a `[30%]` drawdown scenario and committed to not selling into it. Read his own words back to him from the behavioural-risk assessment.
4. **Ran the numbers with him.** Current unrealised loss: `[X%]` of portfolio. If we sell today and markets recover 20% in 6 months (historical base rate for `[specific event]` post-sell-offs), loss-realised = `[$Y]`. Ran the alternative: hold, add modestly to equity if comfortable. Showed the difference in expected outcome.
5. **Offered a smaller action he could take to reduce anxiety.** Agreed to move 5% of portfolio from equity to short-dated T-bills, specifically to give him psychological buffer — "not selling at the bottom, but dialling down the exposure that's hurting you." Small enough not to materially damage long-term plan.
6. **Scheduled daily brief check-ins for the next 10 business days.** 5-minute calls, no pressure to trade — just "are we still on the plan?"

## Result

He did not sell the portfolio. The 5% equity-reduction was the only trade executed. Market recovered `[%]` over the following 6 months; his portfolio fully recovered and went on to new highs 14 months later. Had he sold at the bottom as originally requested, realised losses would have been approximately `[US$X]` — materially damaging to his long-term plan. 18 months later he referred his business partner to me (`[US$Y]` additional AUM). He explicitly thanked me for "not letting me sell at the worst moment."

## What this demonstrates

**Resilience & Composure** — personal capacity to stay calm when the client is emotional, and to structure a disciplined conversation rather than react; **Relationship & Trust** — willingness to hear fear fully before responding, and to reference the documented plan as shared authority rather than personal opinion; **Judgement** — the small 5% reduction as a psychologically effective compromise rather than an all-or-nothing stance.
