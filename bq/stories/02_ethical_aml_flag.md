## Situation

A long-standing client (~`[X year]` relationship, `[US$YM]` AUM) requested an inbound wire of `[US$8M]` from an offshore account I hadn't previously seen in his profile. His stated source-of-funds narrative: "distribution from a family investment company in [jurisdiction]." On initial review, two things didn't add up: the sending institution was in a jurisdiction with no apparent family-business link, and the client pushed back firmly when I asked for supporting documentation, calling the questions "unnecessary given our history."

## Task

Determine whether this was a legitimate reluctance (client believes relationship tenure should bypass process) or a material red flag requiring escalation. Balance due diligence with relationship preservation; avoid tipping-off.

## Action

1. **Didn't bend on the documentation ask.** Framed it as "standard process for inbounds of this size — applies equally to new and long-standing clients; I'm required to document it consistently." Respectful but firm.
2. **Separate client meeting, not on the phone.** Requested face-to-face so I could read his reaction and give him space to explain more. He was more cooperative in person but still vague on specifics.
3. **Ran cross-checks in parallel.** Sanctions screening on the sending entity, adverse-media search on the jurisdiction, corporate-registry look-up on the "family investment company" name. Two hits on the corporate registry suggested the entity had changed ownership 18 months earlier in a way his narrative didn't reference.
4. **Escalated internally before the client conversation went further.** Briefed my team head and the compliance officer same day. Did not process the inbound. Did not discuss the escalation with the client (tipping-off discipline).
5. **Followed compliance instructions.** The review process took `[X weeks]`. I maintained normal service with the client during this period, answered his operational questions, and did not hint at the review.

## Result

Compliance concluded the inbound had material red flags and filed an STR with STRO. The `[US$8M]` was never credited to the account. Ultimately the relationship was formally exited `[X months]` later following a compliance-led decision. The bank avoided what post-hoc investigation suggested was `[describe without specificity: e.g. connection to proceeds of a regional fraud matter that emerged publicly 12 months later]`. My file memo and contemporaneous documentation of the review were cited as evidence of appropriate CDD diligence in the subsequent supervisory review.

**Integrity / Ethics** — willingness to apply CDD discipline even with a long-standing client who expects relationship deference; **Regulatory Awareness** — application of MAS 626 enhanced-CDD expectations, SOF documentation discipline, and tipping-off protocol; **Commercial maturity** — accepting the loss of a AUM relationship as the right outcome versus operating under red-flag risk.
