# Commercial / Initiative: Cold-started a referral chain via one satisfied client

> **Template — personalise with your specifics before using.** Replace all `[bracketed]` placeholders with real details from your career.

## Situation

Early in `[20XX]` I realised my pipeline was too dependent on bank-originated leads and my personal first-degree network, which was substantially tapped. I had one client — `[describe: e.g. a 52-year-old industrial business owner, US$8M AUM, 3-year relationship, genuinely satisfied based on NPS and spontaneous feedback]` — who I believed had a network of peers with similar wealth profiles. But I had never asked him for introductions; in SG culture, aggressive referral-asking can come across badly.

## Task

Develop a referral pipeline from this satisfied client without damaging the relationship, without making him feel used, and without the awkward "can you introduce me to your network?" cold-ask.

## Action

1. **Created value first, asked nothing.** Organised a small dinner (6 people) around a topic genuinely useful to his circle — "cross-border succession planning for family businesses". Asked him to invite 1–2 peers he thought would benefit. The dinner was substantive, not sales. Invited a specialist lawyer to present; I was present as moderator, not pitch-person.
2. **Followed up with actual value post-dinner.** Each attendee received: a written summary of the key takeaways, a specific piece of research relevant to their situation, and nothing that looked like a sales pitch.
3. **Tracked the attendees' responses** — two expressed interest in a follow-up conversation; one of them specifically asked about banking options. The third attendee didn't engage but later referred his brother.
4. **Handled the first follow-up call carefully.** Framed it as "you mentioned interest in X — happy to share perspective" not "let me pitch my bank." If the prospect wasn't genuinely engaged, I let it go without pressure.
5. **Repeated the dinner pattern 2x over the year** with different topics relevant to different sub-segments of his network.
6. **At no point did I overtly ask him for introductions.** He offered. I also hosted one smaller non-topic dinner (4 people, just good conversation) that was purely relationship-building for him.
7. **Recognised his contribution.** At year-end, sent him a thoughtful, non-extravagant gift with a note acknowledging his trust — not a quid-pro-quo but a genuine thank-you for enabling the introductions.

## Result

From the original single client's direct network, I onboarded `[3 new clients]` over 14 months totalling `[US$15M]` AUM. From second-degree referrals (his network's networks, via people I met through him), another `[2 clients]` totalling `[US$8M]`. The original client's AUM grew `[US$3M]` over the same period through fresh liquidity. Total direct and indirect AUM attributable to the first client's network: `[US$26M]` over 14 months — the highest-return relationship in my book by multiplier.

## What this demonstrates

**Commercial Initiative** — proactively built a pipeline rather than relying on inbound leads; **Relationship & Trust** — treated the client's network as something to add value to, not extract from, which is why he offered the introductions rather than being asked; **Judgement** — the topic-dinner format that positioned me as a provider of genuinely useful perspective rather than a salesperson at the door; **Cultural Fit** — respecting the SG / Asian cultural norm of indirect invitation over direct asking.
