# Relationship / Resilience: Difficult UHNW with unrealistic expectations

## Situation

A UHNW client (`[US$XM]` AUM, Aggressive profile, `[X years]` relationship) had been consistently dissatisfied for 6 months. He expected the portfolio to deliver >15% annualised despite a Balanced-Growth mandate targeting 7–9%, and benchmarked himself against a friend who held concentrated single-names in the year's best-performing sector. Every quarterly review became a heated conversation. He'd begun threatening to "find someone who can actually deliver returns," and at one point asked to shift `[US$YM]` to a private-credit fund he'd read about in the news.

## Task

Either reset his expectations to a sustainable basis, or determine whether the relationship was irreparable and should be exited — but not quietly underperform into a complaint 12 months later.

## Action

1. **Requested a dedicated 90-minute meeting.** Not another quarterly review. Framed it as: "I'd like to talk about what success looks like for this portfolio, separate from performance numbers."
2. **Walked him through the math honestly.** Compared his portfolio's actual return (`[X%]`) to a realistic "if we'd done concentrated single-name bets" distribution: 10% of such bets had his friend's outperformance, 60% had returns below his current portfolio, 30% had material drawdowns. Showed him the survivor-bias of the friend's story.
3. **Re-presented the Investment Policy Statement.** What we committed to, what was his risk profile choice, what the realistic outcomes were over 5- and 10-year horizons.
4. **Asked him the hard question.** "If I were to change the mandate to match your return expectations, I'd be recommending concentrated equity positions that could drawdown 50%. Would that match what you want?" He said no.
5. **Proposed a structural change he would own.** A 5% satellite sleeve for his tactical conviction trades, clearly ringfenced as "your picks, your risk," with the core preserved. Gave him agency without putting the core at risk.
6. **Documented everything in writing.** Wrote a summary of the meeting, his agreements, the new sleeve structure; sent to him and retained in file. This both clarified expectations and created a suitability audit trail.

## Result

Relationship stabilised within 2 meetings. The 5% satellite sleeve became his engagement channel; some trades worked, some didn't, and he learned the discipline of sizing. Over the next 18 months the conversation shifted from "why aren't you beating markets" to "what's next for the satellite." AUM grew `[US$YM]` as he consolidated another account to me, cumulative 30-month relationship yielded `[US$ZM]` incremental AUM. He later referred his nephew (`[US$WM]` AUM).

## What this demonstrates

**Relationship & Trust** — willingness to have an honest conversation rather than accommodate unrealistic expectations; **Resilience** — personal capacity to handle a difficult relationship without either capitulating or writing it off; **Commercial Judgement** — the small satellite sleeve as a structural solution that preserved the core while giving the client agency.
