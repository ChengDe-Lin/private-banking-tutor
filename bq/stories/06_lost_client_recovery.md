# Commercial / Resilience: Re-won a client who had left for a competitor

## Situation

A client I'd had for `[4 years]` (`[US$XM]` AUM, core relationship, diversified portfolio) moved the bulk of his assets to a competitor bank `[describe trigger: e.g. a specific RM at a peer bank aggressively pursued him with a structured-product pitch and committed to a specific IPO allocation]`. He left with approximately `[US$18M]` of `[US$20M]` AUM, leaving a residual position as a professional courtesy. It was a painful loss — I had believed the relationship was strong.

## Task

Decide whether to (a) accept the loss and redirect effort elsewhere, (b) immediately counter-pitch and risk looking desperate, or (c) play a longer game — respect his decision, maintain the courtesy residual relationship, and watch for a re-engagement opportunity. I chose (c), with explicit acknowledgement it might take 12+ months to materialise or never materialise.

## Action

1. **Professional, non-emotional exit conversation.** When he told me he was moving, I thanked him for the relationship, asked specifically what had driven the decision (to learn, not to argue), and wished him well. No bitter undertone; no "you'll regret it" hint.
2. **Maintained the residual relationship genuinely.** Quarterly check-ins, sent him relevant research, didn't mention the competitor bank. Kept the door open without pushing.
3. **Tracked publicly-available signals.** His LinkedIn; any company events he attended; our mutual contacts' updates on his situation. I was watchful but not stalker-ish.
4. **Specific moment of opportunity — Month 8.** The IPO he'd been promised by the competitor didn't come through; he'd mentioned in passing that the RM had gone quiet. I timed a meeting invitation not on the IPO issue but on an unrelated topic relevant to him (a piece of research I genuinely thought he'd value).
5. **Listened in that meeting.** He brought up his dissatisfaction voluntarily. I didn't pile on — didn't criticise the competitor, didn't say "I told you so." Just asked questions about what he was looking for.
6. **Proposed a narrow, specific re-engagement.** Not "come back with everything"; just "let me manage this specific piece" — `[US$3M]` IG bond portfolio with a specific mandate. Low-friction reentry point.
7. **Delivered cleanly on the narrow mandate.** Over the next 6 months, I was deliberately unobtrusive but consistent on that `[US$3M]`.
8. **Month 18: he consolidated back.** Moved the full `[US$18M]` back to me from the competitor; total became `[US$22M]` including fresh inflows.

## Result

Re-won `[US$18M]` AUM that had been lost 18 months prior; total relationship grew from `[US$20M]` pre-departure to `[US$22M]` post-return, plus `[US$5M]` of additional assets over the following year. The re-engagement taught him the costs of chasing product-pitches (he estimated `[US$Xk]` of lost returns from 18 months at the competitor). Our relationship became measurably stronger afterward — he specifically valued that I hadn't tried to poach him back aggressively.

## What this demonstrates

**Resilience** — personal capacity to handle a significant professional loss without it damaging the ongoing residual relationship; **Commercial Judgement** — the long-game patience and the narrow re-entry mandate as a lower-risk re-engagement; **Relationship** — the non-bitter professionalism during the exit that left the door genuinely open.
