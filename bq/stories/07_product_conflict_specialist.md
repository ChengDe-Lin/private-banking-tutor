# Judgement / Collaboration: Disagreed with a product specialist on suitability

## Situation

A product specialist from our structured-products team recommended a specific autocallable note for a client of mine (`[describe: e.g. a 60-year-old widow, US$XM AUM, Balanced mandate, limited structured-product experience]`). The note offered an attractive coupon but had a worst-of basket with three moderately correlated underlyings and a knock-in barrier that, in my read, did not fit the client's risk tolerance or her expressed preference for products she could explain back. The specialist was senior, respected, and the trade was commercially attractive to the desk.

## Task

Push back on a specialist recommendation when I had the suitability view, without appearing to undermine the specialist publicly, and without accommodating a trade I thought was wrong for the client.

## Action

1. **Raised the concern privately first, not in a client meeting.** Went to the specialist: "I want to walk through this recommendation for `[client]` — I have a concern about the worst-of basket composition and the knock-in probability given her profile." Asked questions, didn't lead with "I disagree."
2. **Walked through the specific mismatch.** Her IPS target equity exposure ~30%; the embedded short-put in this autocallable on the three-name basket would add material equity-delta on top of her existing equity exposure. Her knock-in scenarios could deliver shares of names she doesn't understand. Her stated preference: products she can explain.
3. **Listened to the specialist's response.** He made valid points about the coupon level relative to alternatives, the historical drawdown on the basket being within her tolerance. Conceded some of his points.
4. **Proposed an alternative.** Instead of the complex autocallable, a simpler single-underlying ELN on a name she owns and understands, with smaller notional, shorter tenor, higher knock-in buffer. Same structural family, lower complexity, better fit.
5. **Agreed the client meeting approach.** Both of us present, walked through the original idea, I flagged the suitability concern, specialist presented the simpler alternative. Client chose the simpler alternative, `[US$500k]` notional, shorter tenor.
6. **Followed up afterward.** Thanked the specialist for his flexibility, and documented in the suitability file the reasoning and the alternative chosen. Avoided making the disagreement public or political.

## Result

Client executed the simpler ELN (`[US$500k]` notional, 6-month, ~6.5% coupon). Product delivered as expected — no knock-in, coupon paid at maturity. Commercial outcome similar to the original recommendation but with suitability much better aligned. The specialist and I continued to work together on subsequent recommendations with a stronger process: I'd pre-discuss suitability concerns with him privately, he'd refine the proposal, we'd present a single view to the client. Over the following year our joint win-rate on structured product sales to my book improved `[X%]` — the pre-alignment produced better-fit products. Avoided a potential suitability issue that could have materialised if the original note had knocked in.

## What this demonstrates

**Judgement** — willingness to challenge a senior specialist's recommendation based on suitability grounds, rather than deferring because of hierarchy; **Collaboration** — handling the disagreement privately first, treating the specialist as a partner rather than an adversary, and building a better joint process going forward; **Suitability Discipline** — applying the ABS PB Code "reasonable basis for recommendation" framework rigorously.
