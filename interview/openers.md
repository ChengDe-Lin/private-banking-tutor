# Interview Openers — Self-Intro, "Why", and Closing Questions

> **This is the frame of every interview.** Before you talk about products or cases, you have 3–5 minutes of openers: self-introduction, "why PB," "why this bank," "why you." How you answer these determines whether the interviewer listens carefully to the rest or is already forming a polite rejection. This page is the script architecture — not the words (those must be yours) but the structure, the emphasis, the common traps, and the bank-specific "why this firm" angles.

## 直覺理解

面試的前 5 分鐘決定後 55 分鐘被怎麼聽。**自我介紹 (self-intro)** 是第一印象的畫框；**"Why PB / Why this bank / Why you" 三問**是雇主判斷「這個人有沒有搞清楚自己要什麼」的測試。對於從台灣 / 香港 / 中國轉到新加坡的候選人，還多了一道**"Why Singapore / How long will you stay"**——這題答不好會被歸類成「用 SG 當跳板」，培訓 ROI 算不過來直接淘汰。內容要誠實、具體、有 PB 味道——不是泛泛的「我很努力」（那是 HR screen 在聽的），而是「我在上一份工作裡遇過什麼客戶情境、當時怎麼處理、那個經驗讓我想轉 PB 的什麼角色」。最後「你有問題要問我嗎？」不是 polite closer，而是最後一道題，答不出會 -20 分。

---

## 1. Self-introduction — the 60–90 second opener

**Purpose:** Orient the interviewer. Give them the mental file-folder they'll use to parse the rest of the conversation.

**Structure (in this order, not long):**

1. **Who you are today** (10–15 sec) — current role, tenure, firm, what you're responsible for. One line.
2. **Journey arc in one sentence** (10–15 sec) — how you got here, the through-line that led to PB.
3. **Signature strength** (15–20 sec) — one specific, verifiable capability you bring (not adjectives — something you've *done*). Ideally linked to a client situation, without naming clients.
4. **What you're looking for next** (10–15 sec) — why now, why PB, why specifically this conversation. One line.
5. **Segue** (5 sec) — "happy to go deeper on any of that."

**Total: 60–90 seconds. Any longer and you've lost the room.**

### Template (personalise)

> "I've spent the last `[X years]` in `[role / function]` at `[current firm]`, most recently covering `[segment / geography]`. My career started in `[origin]` and moved through `[steps]`, and the through-line has been `[the thing that connects the dots — client-facing discipline / cross-border capability / numerical rigor / relationship depth]`. The capability I'd point to most is `[one specific strength]` — for example, I `[one-line achievement, anonymised]`. I'm now looking specifically for a role where `[two or three things: client ownership / investment depth / platform / growth opportunity]`, and that's why I wanted to talk to `[target firm]` about the `[role]` specifically."

### Common traps

- **Too long.** 3 minutes of self-intro = you've used your airtime on scaffolding and lost the chance to discuss anything interesting.
- **Too generic.** "I'm hard-working, detail-oriented, relationship-driven" — any adjective that applies to every candidate adds nothing.
- **No through-line.** A CV read-out that jumps between roles without a reason wastes the "why now PB" set-up.
- **Under-selling specific signature capability.** You need one concrete thing — don't hide it under modesty.
- **Adjective-stacking.** "I'm passionate about markets and care deeply about clients" — the interviewer zones out.

### Finance-background candidate specifics

- If you're a finance / economics major moving into PB, the through-line usually runs: **academic discipline → analytical role → client-facing role**. Use the through-line to show you understand the progression from technical depth to commercial application.
- If you have prior PB or financial-advisory experience, the question is about what makes you want to *this* role rather than *more* of the same.
- If you're transitioning from another industry, the interviewer needs to believe the move is deliberate, not a default after other options closed. Be explicit about what triggered the shift.

## 2. "Why private banking?"

**What's actually being tested:** (a) do you understand the job, or is "PB" just a label for "a bank job with bigger numbers"; (b) have you thought about whether you'd be good at it.

**Weak answers the interviewer has heard 1,000 times:**

- "I like working with high-net-worth clients" — so does everyone applying.
- "I want to combine investments and relationships" — true of every advisory role.
- "PB offers more autonomy" — hearsay; doesn't show you understand the trade-offs.

**Stronger answer structure — four pillars:**

| Pillar | What to say | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Client-complexity draw** | UHNW clients have multi-jurisdictional, multi-generational problems that can't be solved with one product — the intellectual puzzle is rich | Shows you know the work is more than "selling products" |
| **Time horizon / relationship depth** | Multi-decade relationships let you see the full arc — wealth accumulation, transition, distribution, succession. That's rare in finance | Shows you value the non-transactional nature |
| **Commercial ownership** (for RM track) | Running a book is closest to running a business within a bank — revenue, costs, risk, all yours | Signals commercial maturity |
| **Technical breadth** (for IC track) | Markets, portfolio construction, products, client psychology, regulation — the range is rare | Signals intellectual ambition |

**Honest answer template:**

> "Three things. First, the complexity — UHNW clients have multi-jurisdictional, multi-generational situations where the interesting work is framing the problem, not picking the product. I've seen this in `[specific context]` and it's the part of the job I find genuinely engaging. Second, the time horizon — PB relationships run 10–20 years, which means you see the full cycle of wealth, not just a transaction window. I want to do work where longevity of relationship is a measured outcome, not a side-effect. Third, `[RM track: commercial ownership — running a book is the closest thing to running a business inside a bank; IC track: the technical range — markets, portfolio, products, regulation, psychology — rare to get all five layers in one role]`. Those three, together, make PB the specific place I want to be — not just 'finance' or 'wealth'."

### If asked "why not IB / AM / corporate banking?"

- **vs IB:** transaction-cycle is too short; you solve a problem and move on. PB is relationship-cycle, which you want.
- **vs AM:** at AM you optimise a mandate; you don't hear the client story. PB combines the portfolio layer with the client layer.
- **vs Corporate Banking:** corporate is transactional lending; PB is holistic wealth — you're working with the *person*, not the company. Different problem.

Be careful not to speak dismissively of other roles — interviewers are often alumni of them and have friends there.

## 3. "Why this bank?"

**What's being tested:** did you bother to research the firm specifically, or is this CV #47.

**You must give at least 3 firm-specific reasons.** Generic "great reputation, strong platform" is a fail. Specificity = caring.

### Bank-by-bank positioning cheat sheet (as of 2024–2025)

> Use this to seed your research; verify current state before the interview. For deeper context see [`../industry.md`](../industry.md).

**UBS Global Wealth Management**

- #1 globally by PB AUM after the Credit Suisse acquisition (March 2023). Integrating the CS book across APAC is the live strategic story through 2024–2026.
- Strengths: scale, global advisory reach, deep CIO office, structured-product franchise, PE / alts platform access.
- Angle for "why UBS": you want scale + integrated platform; you see the CS integration as opportunity (growing book, new clients onboarding) rather than chaos.
- Watch: integration friction is real — have a take on it without being negative.

**JP Morgan Private Bank**

- Top-tier UHNW focus; institutional-quality research; strong US-cross-border capability; PE / alts access.
- Angle: you want the most institutional-feel PB — the one closest to the IB / AM quality. Right for clients whose needs touch markets in ways other PBs can't serve.
- Compensation often stronger at senior levels; selective hiring.

**Bank of Singapore (OCBC)**

- Asia-focused regional flagship; integrated with OCBC corporate bank for UHNW business-banking cross-sell.
- Angle: you want Asia-native, SG-origin scale with regional booking depth. Right if your book is Asia-anchored.
- Culture: locally-rooted, less global-corporate than UBS / JPM.

**Citi Private Bank**

- UHNW / family office emphasis; global markets access; institutional-adjacent.
- Angle: you want a PB that thinks like a FO platform — direct market access, PE co-invest, institutional-style servicing.

**DBS Private Bank**

- SG #1 local bank; investing heavily in digital advisory (iWealth / digital platform); growing regional coverage.
- Angle: you believe SG / Asia is where PB growth happens, and you want a platform doubling down on digital + service innovation.

**HSBC Global Private Banking**

- Asia / Middle-East emphasis; global footprint; strong China / HK corridor; post-2022–2023 repositioning.
- Angle: you want Asia-to-global platform; cross-border corridor business (mainland China wealth offshoring) is a specific strength.

**Deutsche Bank Private Bank / International Private Bank**

- Germany-anchored; smaller APAC book; strong European / German UHNW; structured-product capability.
- Angle: European-cross-border; structured products; or specifically you have German / European client base.

**BNP Paribas Wealth Management**

- European UHNW strong, growing APAC; France / Monaco heritage.
- Angle: if your book is European or French-speaking; otherwise speak to the APAC growth story.

**Julius Baer**

- Pure-play Swiss PB — no IB attached; independent-advisor positioning; ~30%+ APAC book.
- Angle: you want a firm where the PB *is* the business, not a sleeve attached to a trading operation. Advisory independence is the pitch.

**Pictet Wealth Management**

- Swiss independent; UHNW / FO bias; highly selective hiring; long relationships; conservative positioning.
- Angle: you value conservatism, low turnover, and multi-generation relationships over quarterly revenue targeting.

**Lombard Odier**

- Similar to Pictet — Swiss independent, UHNW FO focus; long culture of private ownership.
- Angle: similar — you want Swiss independent, FO-focused.

**LGT Bank**

- Liechtenstein princely-house-owned; long horizon; alternatives-strong; growing APAC.
- Angle: family-owned means no quarterly pressure; philosophically aligned with UHNW patience; strong alts.

**Goldman Sachs PWM (SG) / Morgan Stanley Wealth (SG)**

- Small, senior book; tight capital-markets integration.
- Angle: you want the institutional-markets quality at a smaller / more senior book.

**Standard Chartered Private Bank**

- Emerging-market footprint (Asia / Middle-East / Africa); integrated with SC corporate.
- Angle: EM corridor business; specific EM client segments.

### The three-reason formula

Pick **three** specific things. Mix platform + positioning + personal fit:

1. **One platform / capability reason** — "your CIO office's work on `[specific topic / call]` is the kind of depth I want access to"; or "your `[specific product / booking centre / credit appetite]` fits my book's needs"; or "your family-office capability / Asia-desk / US-cross-border strength matches what I'm trying to build."
2. **One positioning / strategic reason** — "your post-CS integration is creating the scale moment I want to be part of"; or "your Asia-growth strategy in `[segment]` is where I think PB is going"; or "your family-owned independence means I can have 15-year client conversations without revenue-quarter pressure."
3. **One personal-fit reason** — "I've spoken with `[name X, name Y]` at your firm and the culture matches how I want to work"; or "the `[specific team / head of APAC / segment lead]` has built the kind of franchise I want to learn from"; or "your internal mobility track from `[X]` to `[Y]` maps to where I see my career."

**All three should be researchable, not generic.** If you can't name a specific team, product capability, or public statement from the firm, the answer is too thin.

### Trap answers

- **"Your reputation speaks for itself."** Lazy; says nothing.
- **"I've always wanted to work at `[firm]`."** Unless you have a 10-year specific narrative, it sounds desperate.
- **"Your compensation is excellent."** True for many candidates, but saying it signals money-first orientation.
- **"Your culture is great."** Everyone says this; without specifics, it's noise.

## 4. "Why Singapore?" / "How long will you stay?" — for international candidates

**Who this section is for.** Candidates relocating to SG from Taiwan, Hong Kong, mainland China, Malaysia, Indonesia, or further afield. If you are a SG citizen or PR, skim this — it will still come up, but the burden of proof is lower.

**What's actually being tested:**
- **Commitment.** PB hire-to-productivity is 12–24 months. A firm spending training and platform on you needs to believe you will be there in year 3+. "3 years then back home" is an interview-killer; be honest but structured.
- **Market literacy.** Do you understand *why* SG is the booking-centre story of the decade (MAS 13O/13U, VCC, post-HK FO migration, regulatory quality), or is "SG is a nice place to live" the extent of your thinking?
- **Logistics reality.** Visa status (EP / S-pass / PR / citizen), family circumstances, cost-of-living readiness — the CIO will want to confirm you've thought past the first six months.
- **Cultural adaptability.** Taiwan is mono-ethnic Mandarin; SG is multi-ethnic English-primary with Chinese / Indian / Malay / Eurasian communities plus a mainland China / HK / Indo / TW / European expat layer. Can you read the room?

### Weak answers the interviewer has heard 1,000 times

- **"Singapore is a great place to live / food / safe / clean."** Lifestyle answers signal you haven't thought about the work.
- **"Singapore is the safe haven for Asian wealth."** True but generic. Every candidate says it.
- **"I've always wanted to work in Singapore."** Hard to verify; sounds vague without a specific decision-moment.
- **"3–5 years and I'll see."** Kills the interview. Firms are hiring for 5–10 years.

### The four-pillar strong answer

| Pillar | What to say | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Market structure** | SG is where Asian UHNW is consolidating — MAS 13O/13U regime, VCC framework, post-HK migration, regulatory quality. The work I want to do is increasingly booked here. | Shows you know the industry trend, not just the city |
| **Career density** | SG has Asia's deepest bench of senior PB talent (every major bank, every Swiss independent, serious EAM layer). For a candidate at my stage, 5–10 years in that concentration is compounding I can't get in Taipei / KL / Jakarta. | Frames SG as a career investment, not a lifestyle choice |
| **Fit** | My Mandarin + English working profile and Greater-China cultural background matches the client segment the target firm serves. | Turns your international origin into an asset, not a liability |
| **Commitment** | I've done the logistics research, I've rejected specific alternatives in my home market, and I'm here for a 5–10 year horizon — not a rotation. Be specific about visa / family. | Directly addresses the retention-risk question interviewers are silently running |

### Template answer (personalise; do NOT read verbatim)

> "I chose Singapore for three reasons and I'm committing to a 5-to-10-year horizon here, not a 2-to-3-year rotation. **First, the market structure.** Asian UHNW wealth has been consolidating in SG since 2019–2020 — HK's political shift, the 13O/13U regime, the VCC framework, MAS regulatory quality. The work I want to do — helping Greater China UHNW families structure cross-border wealth — is increasingly booked in SG, and that trend isn't reversing. **Second, the learning density.** SG has the deepest concentration of senior PB talent in Asia — every major bank, every Swiss independent, a serious EAM layer. For a candidate at my stage, being in that room for 5–10 years is compounding I cannot get in Taipei. **Third, the fit.** My Mandarin-and-English working profile plus my Greater-China cultural background matches `[target firm]`'s client segment specifically; I'm not arriving in SG hoping to build Greater-China exposure — I come with it. On commitment: I've done the logistics — `[visa status / EP track / family support]` — and I've turned down `[X specific alternative in TW / HK]` because this is a multi-year decision not a rotation."

### "How long will you stay?" — the follow-up drill

This is often a second-round question after the CIO has already formed a good-enough impression. The honest answer is the winning answer:

- **Right:** "5 to 10 years minimum, with my current plan being to build a full career here. My family situation supports it; my visa path is clear; my career math works here in a way it doesn't elsewhere."
- **Wrong:** "Forever" (implausible, sounds desperate). "3–5 years" (too short, telegraphs you're using SG as a pit-stop). Vague non-commitment ("we'll see how it goes") — screams retention risk.
- **If family circumstances genuinely could pull you back:** say so, briefly and framed forward. "My parents are in Taiwan; I visit quarterly; I don't foresee a move back unless a specific health event, and I would flag that long before it became a retention issue for the firm." Honesty about constraints beats over-promising.

### Taiwanese-candidate-specific context

- **TW's US$5M annual outward-remittance quota** (CBC rule) is why TW UHNW offshore wealth flows into SG (and historically HK, now increasingly SG post-2020). Knowing this signals market literacy.
- **TW estate tax (20% on worldwide estates for TW-domiciled individuals)** drives TW UHNW to use SG booking centres + offshore structures for succession. If you know the drivers, the firm's Greater-China orientation matches yours.
- **Overseas-income minimum tax (海外所得最低稅負)** for TW residents is a specific constraint TW clients ask about; being able to say "this is on my radar and I'd flag it on the client conversation, not dodge it" is credibility-generating.
- **Cultural-overlap-with-caveats:** SG and TW Chinese communities share roots, but SG's multi-ethnic English-first workplace is different from TW's Mandarin monoculture. Show you've thought about the adjustment — SG colleagues expect working English in meetings, not Mandarin-default.

### FAQ of follow-ups to prepare

| Question | Short-form answer |
|---|---|
| **Why not HK?** | Post-2020 political shift, regulatory uncertainty, FO outflows into SG. For a multi-year commitment at this career stage, SG's trajectory is the one I'm backing. |
| **Why not London?** | Time zone and distance from Asian UHNW clients; tax drag on your income; the growth of wealth in the next decade is Asia, not Europe. |
| **Why not stay in Taipei?** | Market is structurally small (CBC outflow quota means UHNW wealth is offshore); PB career density is 10–20× larger in SG; the clients I want to serve don't book in TW. |
| **Why not Shanghai?** | Capital-account, political, and regulatory uncertainty make it the wrong base for a career serving Greater-China private wealth. |
| **Will you miss home?** | Yes — and I've planned for it (family visits, network via video). This is not news to me; I've already done the cost-benefit and SG wins. |
| **Can you handle SG cost of living?** | Yes — planned accommodation at `[X level]`, salary range supports it, I've done the math. |
| **What's your visa status?** | Be ready to state: EP-eligible candidate / current pass holder / PR track / citizen. Employer cares about renewability and timeline. |

### Traps to avoid

- **Bashing Taiwan / HK / home market.** "The market there is too small for my ambitions" is OK; "I'm escaping Taiwan" is not. Interviewers respect ambition; they distrust people who talk badly about where they came from.
- **Over-claiming SG knowledge.** If you haven't lived here, don't pretend you have. Say: "I've visited N times, spent Y weeks in the last year, read the MAS guidelines, spoken to `[specific people]`. That's my depth so far — I know I'll be building daily from Day 1."
- **Ducking the retention question.** If you have a real time-horizon constraint (spouse's contract, parental health), naming it honestly and framing your work around it earns more trust than a generic "I'm here long-term."

## 5. "Why you?" / "Why should we hire you?"

**What's being tested:** do you understand what the job actually requires, and can you credibly match yourself to it.

**Structure — the three-pillar answer:**

| Pillar | What to say | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Fit to segment / region** | "I bring a specific segment or geographic strength you're trying to build" | Named segment + examples |
| **Product / commercial / technical** | "I have the technical or commercial depth you need" | Specific skills + results |
| **Cultural / platform fit** | "I understand how your firm works and can contribute quickly" | References, awareness of firm specifics |

### Don't list; sequence.

Weak: "I'm good at relationships, commercially aware, technically strong, and culturally fit." (checklist-read, unmemorable)

Strong: **"The core reason is — `[one pillar lead, 20 sec]`. On top of that — `[second pillar, 15 sec]`. And — `[third pillar, 10 sec]`. All of that says I can hit the ground running in a way a generic strong candidate might not."**

### For lateral RM candidates — book-transfer framing

The "why you" for a lateral RM is substantially about *what you can bring in AUM*. Be specific but defensible (see [`../business_plan/template.md`](../business_plan/template.md)):

> "Beyond my own capabilities, I bring a transferable book of approximately `[US$XM]` — `[N]` core relationships plus `[M]` transactional ones. Of that, `[US$YM]` I'd class as high-confidence to follow me to your platform given how we've talked, and `[US$ZM]` as medium-confidence. I'm comfortable defending those numbers name-by-name. On top of that, my network `[specific COI — lawyers, accountants, M&A advisors, alumni]` gives me Year-1 acquisition channels independent of the book transfer — my estimate for Year-1 new-AUM is `[US$WM]`. Over the first three years I'd target `[US$TotalM]` AUM at your platform."

**Do NOT over-claim.** Interviewers have seen enough business plans to know when transferable-% is fantasy.

### For IC candidates — specialist-skill framing

> "Three things. I bring `[specific product / asset-class / portfolio-construction depth]` — particularly around `[specific area where you're above-market]`. I have a track record supporting RMs across `[segment breadth]` which matters for a coverage role. And I have a developed market view that's grounded in `[framework / CIO discipline / data]` — for example, my current take on `[specific current asset-class view]` is `[specific thesis]`. I'd be happy to walk through that in detail."

## 6. "Why are you leaving your current firm?"

**What's being tested:** do you handle the conversation with dignity, without bashing the prior firm.

**Golden rule:** positive framing, not negative. Even if the real reason is "my boss is awful" or "the bonus was cut," the *stated* reason should be something about the future at the new firm, not the past at the old.

**Canonical safe patterns:**

- **"Looking for next-stage growth I can't get at current firm."** — platform / scale / international / specific capability.
- **"Specific capability the new firm has that fits my book's needs."** — e.g. "my clients are increasingly asking for `[capability]`, and you have that infrastructure."
- **"Strategic direction of current firm no longer matches my segment focus."** — say this only if it's defensible and polite.
- **"Seeking to transition from `[X role / segment]` to `[Y role / segment]`."** — a developmental move, e.g. RM → senior RM, junior to senior segment.
- **"Personal / family reason."** — moving cities, family logistics; keep brief.

**What NEVER to say:**

- **"My manager is difficult."** — even if true, signals you can't work with difficult people.
- **"Bonus was disappointing."** — money-first optics.
- **"Politics."** — vague; suggests you're part of it.
- **"I was made redundant."** — if true, handle carefully: "my team was restructured; I'm looking for the next right fit."
- **"I hate the firm."** — obvious; but seen it in real interviews.

If your departure was actually a redundancy or involuntary: be honest but framed forward. "My segment was restructured; my role was eliminated. I'm looking for the next opportunity where I can rebuild a book." Clean, no drama.

### "Why are you leaving after only X months?"

If your tenure is short (<18 months):

- Own it briefly: "Shorter than I'd have liked."
- Give the *structural* reason: role was mis-scoped, platform didn't support client needs, leadership change, strategic pivot.
- Pivot forward: "Which is why I'm being deliberate about this next move — I want a role and a platform that will be a long-term home."

Avoid sounding like a job-hopper; interviewer needs to believe the new role would be multi-year.

## 7. "Do you have any questions for us?" — the closing question

**Non-negotiable: have 5–7 good questions prepared.** Answering "no, I think you've covered everything" is the single biggest unforced error in the closing minutes. This is where the interviewer assesses: (a) are you curious, (b) have you thought about the role, (c) are you interviewing them too — i.e. are you discerning, or desperate.

**Three categories of good questions — mix them:**

### A. Role-specific (2–3 questions)

- "How is success measured for this role in Year 1, Year 2, Year 3?"
- "Who are the two or three clients you'd expect me to have a first conversation with in Week 4?"
- "How is the team structured? Who are the people I'd work with daily?"
- "What's the coverage model — how many RMs does an IC support, typically?"
- "How are deals / trades / specialist introductions handled between RM and product specialists?"
- "What happens when a client wants to move to DPM — how is that commercially treated?"
- "How do you think about retrocessions and trailer fees under the current regulatory direction?"

### B. Strategic / firm-level (2–3 questions)

- "How is your firm positioning post-`[specific industry event — e.g. CS integration, post-1MDB posture reset, family-office inflow]`?"
- "Where do you see your segment growing vs slowing over the next 24 months?"
- "What's the team's 2–3 year investment in `[specific capability — alternatives, digital, FO, specific geography]`?"
- "How does the CIO office actually influence RM-level conversations? What's the governance between house view and RM flexibility?"
- "What's been the most material change in how you onboard or serve clients since `[specific recent regulatory moment]`?"

### C. Personal / cultural (1–2 questions)

- "You've been at the firm `[X years]` — what's kept you? What would you change?"
- "What separates an average RM / IC at your firm from a top performer?"
- "How does the firm support RM / IC development in year 1–3 — formal training, mentoring, rotation options?"
- "How is internal mobility — segment changes, geography changes — treated at the 3–5 year mark?"

### What NOT to ask

- **Compensation** — unless the interviewer introduces it. Wait for the HR stage.
- **Vacation, hours, work-from-home** — signals lifestyle-first orientation; ask HR later.
- **"Do I get the job?"** — desperate.
- **Anything answered on the firm's website** — "how much AUM does your bank have?" — shows you didn't research.
- **Negative questions about competitors** — stays positive about own firm.

### Closing move

When the interviewer starts to wind down:

> "Thank you for the time — I've really enjoyed the conversation. A few specific things stood out that I'd want to explore further, particularly `[specific topic]`. What are the next steps from your side?"

This locks in: (a) appreciation; (b) recall of one specific topic (signals you listened); (c) process question that makes them commit to a timeline.

## Role-specific emphasis

### For RM candidates

- Self-intro should emphasise **client ownership** and **commercial track record**.
- "Why PB" leans on relationship / long-horizon / running a book.
- "Why you" integrates the business-plan narrative (book transfer).
- Questions-to-ask lean toward role clarity (Year 1 success) and book-support (team structure, specialist access).

### For IC candidates

- Self-intro should emphasise **technical depth** and **judgement**.
- "Why PB" leans on intellectual breadth, not commercial ownership.
- "Why you" emphasises specialist capability and market-view rigor.
- Questions-to-ask lean toward investment process (CIO governance, coverage model, how views are formed) and development (mentoring, rotation).

See [`../roles.md`](../roles.md) for the broader distinction.

## Rehearsal discipline

1. **Write each opener in full — in your own words.** Don't memorise the template; memorise the structure, write it in your voice.
2. **Out-loud rehearsal.** Record yourself; listen back; time it. 60–90 seconds is short — you'll feel it.
3. **Different framings for different firms.** Your "why PB" can be the same across firms; "why this bank" must be firm-specific every time.
4. **Update 48 hours before each interview.** News may have happened — integrate it if relevant.
5. **Have 2 sets of questions-to-ask prepared.** One set for a first-round interview (more role-specific), one for a final-round (more strategic / senior).
