## 直覺理解

CW 不是銀行，是**獨立資產管理公司 (EAM / MFO)**。這讓面試的許多答題框架得重新校準——bank PB 的「book transfer + product-shelf depth + NIM」這套語言對 EAM 不適用；EAM 的真正賣點是**中立、不被單一銀行產品架綁住、純 AUM fee 沒有 trailer conflict**。要答好「why CW」，你得能對照出**為什麼不是 bank PB、為什麼不是另一家更大的 EAM (WRISE / Raffles FO)、為什麼不是另一家更老的 EAM (Taurus)**。這頁就是做這個三層對照。

### "Why CW" — three-pillar drafts (personalise with your specifics)

**Pillar 1 — Why EAM over bank PB:**

> "Three reasons. First, post-CS 2023 trust dynamics — UHNW clients increasingly want multi-custody, not single-bank dependency, and CW's positioning aligns with where clients are moving. Second, fee alignment — pure % AUM, no trailer, is the honest answer to every 'are you incentivised to push X?' conversation I've had. Third, career structure — at an EAM stage firm, I can be genuinely generalist, own the client end-to-end, and potentially own part of what I help build rather than being another RM in a matrix."

**Pillar 2 — Why CW specifically (vs other EAMs):**

> "I looked at the SG EAM landscape. CW stands out on three dimensions. One — the founder team: your career from SocGen through UBS to Julius Baer into founding CW signals a specific theory of what major-bank PB fails to deliver at the UHNW segment, and you built CW around that gap. Two — Greater China orientation: your book and network align with the segment I've been serving / want to serve, and alignment matters more at a smaller firm. Three — the booking-centre mix (HK/SG/London/Liechtenstein/Zurich) signals the firm is structurally set up for cross-border UHNW families, not SG-only."

**Pillar 3 — Why you for CW:**

> "For this role specifically, I bring three things. Greater China / Taiwan cultural and language fit for CW's likely client base. Willingness to be generalist — I understand that at an EAM stage firm the role is end-to-end ownership including operations and documentation, not the specialist-supported version at a major bank; I prefer that. And a long-horizon mindset — I'm looking for a 5–10 year home where I can compound a book and a firm, not a 3-year rotation."

## "Why CW over Firm X" — answer drafts

**If Harry asks "How do you see us vs WRISE?":**

> "Different theories of the business. WRISE is building scale — 400 people, multi-hub, explicit tech-forward positioning, aiming for bank-size AUM on an EAM licence. That works if the client values breadth of platform. CW's bet is the opposite — staying intimate, founder-direct, client-relationship deeper because the team is smaller and access to you is real, not funnel-mediated. For the UHNW segment where the client specifically wants to work with the founder, not a matrix, CW wins. WRISE is a fine firm, but the models compete on different axes."

**If Harry asks "Why not just go to Raffles Family Office?":**

> "RFO is the scale-platform version of what CW is becoming. 150 people, dual-HQ, Asia-wide. That's a more institutional experience for both clients and employees. For a client who wants access to a proper MFO platform with multiple cities and more advisory layers, RFO is a good answer. For a client — and an employee — who value founder-direct engagement and the opportunity to shape a firm's culture rather than join a 150-person machine, CW is the better fit. I'm looking for the latter."

**Optional: referencing the 2025 Innovation Awards.** You don't need to lead with this, but if the conversation opens a door:

> "The multiple honours at the 2025 Global Private Banking Innovation Awards stood out to me as an external signal — a 4-year-old firm being recognised that way is usually a platform-building or client-outcome signal that's hard to fake in industry-voted awards. That, plus your own career trajectory, is what made CW specifically the firm I spent most of my research time on."

**If Harry asks "Taurus has been around 15+ years and has US$3B — why not them?":**

> "Taurus is an impressive longevity story and I respect what Mandeep has built. The difference for me is focus. Taurus is globally diversified — clients across 25+ countries, India / Middle East / Europe weight as much as Asia. CW's focus on Greater China maps directly onto the segment I understand and want to serve. Matching client and employee geographic focus is higher-leverage than optimising for firm tenure alone."
