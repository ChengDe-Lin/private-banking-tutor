# RM vs Investment Counselor — Two Different Interviews

> **Read this before the 7-day path.** The two roles share ~70% of the knowledge base (products, regulation, markets, cases) but diverge sharply on *what interviewers actually drill* and *which stories get you hired*. Mis-calibrating your preparation — e.g. a prospective IC over-practising book-transfer / acquisition pitch, or an RM candidate drowning in Greeks minutiae — is the single most common pre-interview failure mode.

## 直覺理解

RM (Relationship Manager) 是**客戶關係的 owner**——扛 AUM、扛新客戶、扛交叉銷售，是商業人員。IC (Investment Counselor / Investment Advisor) 是**投資建議與產品專業的 owner**——不扛個人 AUM，但支援多個 RM、在客戶會議中扮演「投資專家」的角色，內容技術含量比 RM 深。

一句話的差別：**RM 賣關係 (sells relationship)，IC 賣判斷 (sells judgement)**。RM 的面試會壓 commercial / relationship 故事、book transfer plan、難纏客戶案例；IC 的面試會壓市場觀點、組合構建、產品機制、如何挑戰客戶錯誤想法。產品知識兩者都需要，但 IC 要講到 payoff 圖、Greeks 直覺、skew、duration、spread 這一層；RM 要講到「適合哪類客戶、對話怎麼開、怎麼結案」這一層。

## The role distinction in one table

| Dimension | **RM / Senior RM / Director** | **Investment Counselor / Advisor** |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary KPI** | AUM (Assets Under Management), net new money (NNM), revenue | Quality of advisory output; internal RM satisfaction; client engagement on recommendations |
| **AUM target?** | Yes — personal AUM book, typically US$300–800M+ for RM, US$1B+ for Director | No personal AUM — shared coverage of an RM book |
| **Client-facing?** | Primary relationship owner; weekly / monthly contact | On-request, typically in joint meetings with the RM, especially for portfolio reviews, new-product pitches, quarterly market briefs |
| **Revenue attribution** | Directly attributable — commissions, mgt fees, NIM on Lombard | Shared — IC is a cost centre overhead, measured on coverage quality and contribution to RM revenue |
| **Career ladder** | ARM → RM → Sr RM → AD → D → ED → MD | Junior IC → IC → Sr IC → Head of IC (regional / segment); or transition to CIO / Investment Committee |
| **Typical compensation** (as of 2024–2025, SG) | Base US$100–300k + variable scaled with revenue; senior RMs at 30–50% of revenue generated | Base US$120–250k + variable scaled on KPI + platform performance; typically more stable, lower ceiling |
| **Interview emphasis** | 40% commercial / BQ, 30% product, 20% regulation, 10% market | 50% product + portfolio construction + markets, 30% BQ (including specialist-vs-RM stories), 15% regulation, 5% commercial |
| **Business plan required?** | Yes — detailed book-transfer plan, transferable-AUM %, target ROA, Year-1 to Year-3 milestones | No (or very different flavour: a coverage plan rather than an acquisition plan) |
| **Deepest product layer** | "Which client, which product, which conversation" | "How does the payoff work, what's the Greek profile, what's the fair value, what's the pricing vs comparables" |
| **Language of the role** | Commercial, relational, cross-cultural, empathetic, ethical | Technical, precise, opinionated, curious, judgement-orientated |

**Jargon disambiguation.** Some banks use different titles for the same function:

- **RM** (UBS, Credit Suisse-era, Julius Baer) = **Private Banker** (JPM, BOS) = **Relationship Manager** (most Asian PBs). Same function.
- **IC** (UBS, HSBC, DBS) = **Investment Advisor** (JPM, Citi) = **Investment Specialist** = **Portfolio Counselor**. Same function.
- **IC is distinct from PM (Portfolio Manager / DPM Manager)**. PM is the discretionary portfolio manager who takes full authority over a mandate; IC sits in the **advisory** flow where the client decides. The PM path is a separate role entirely (often on the CIO / investment-management side of the house) — don't conflate.

**The case study is the same question in form, different in answer.** Given "US$20M retiree, USD base, wants 5% income":

- **RM answer** leans into client-relationship framing: "First meeting, I'd ask about family structure, liquidity needs, and existing advisors. Here's how I'd open the conversation… Here's the specific product mix I'd pitch in month 1 vs month 3… Here's how I'd handle the inevitable 'is this the right platform?' question."
- **IC answer** leans into portfolio-construction framing: "SAA target 60/35/5 FI/eq/alt. Core 60% USD IG laddered 2–8Y at ~5% YTW, 20% US equity index, 10% SGD cash/SGS for base-currency ballast, 5% S-REIT for yield, 5% IG perp carry sleeve. Here's the duration, credit, FX, and liquidity profile… here's the stress test… here's how I'd present the SAA to the RM and the client in the kickoff meeting."

Both answers are legitimate. Missing the frame will make you sound miscast.

## Role-specific reading paths through this repo

The content in this repo isn't duplicated; it's just read with different emphasis. Two suggested re-orderings of the 7-day study path:

### RM-emphasis path

| Day | Focus | Shift from default path |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | [Derivatives](products/derivatives.md) — **skim Greeks section**; focus on payoff tables and the "when to recommend" column | Lower weight on Greeks mechanics |
| D2 | [Structured products](products/structured.md) — **full read**, especially "who gets hurt" passages | As default |
| D3 | [FI](products/fixed_income.md), [FX](products/fx.md), [Leverage](products/leverage.md) — **leverage first**, because RM books run on Lombard NIM | Leverage priority over product depth |
| D4 | [WM concepts](concepts/wealth_management.md), [cases × 3](cases/), [alternatives](products/alternatives.md) | As default, but spend more time on the **case-study narration style** |
| D5 | **Regulation — full read**, plus [market view](market_view.md), [industry](industry.md) | Regulation is interview-critical when client on-boarding is part of your job |
| **D6** | **[BQ story bank — full read + personalise](bq/) + [business plan template](business_plan/template.md)** | **This is the RM's load-bearing day.** Draft the actual business plan and polish at least 5 STAR stories with real numbers |
| D7 | Mock pass focused on commercial and BQ questions; revisit weakest topic | Heavy on "walk me through your first year" |

**Non-obvious RM prep priorities:**

1. **Book-transfer plan** — the interviewer will drill every line of the transferable-AUM table. For each named client, know why they're a 100% vs 60% vs 20% transferability, and have a back-up plan if the confident names don't follow.
2. **AUM pitch** — a 5-minute pitch: who you are, who your book is, Year-1 AUM, product mix, revenue guidance, why this bank's platform fits. Rehearse it.
3. **Cross-cultural dynamics for Taiwanese / Greater China clients** — family concentration, generational wealth transfer, patriarch-driven decision styles, how you localise in a SG booking centre without losing the relationship.
4. **Knowing when to say "I'll come back to you with the specialist"** — sign of a good RM who doesn't pretend expertise. Don't over-reach on product questions.

### IC-emphasis path

| Day | Focus | Shift from default path |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | [Derivatives](products/derivatives.md) — **full Greeks, parity, payoff math** | Deeper than default |
| D2 | [Structured products](products/structured.md) — **full read + pricing intuition + stress-testing variants** | As default, with extra time |
| D3 | [FI](products/fixed_income.md) **full read including YTW / AT1 / duration math**, [FX](products/fx.md), [equities/funds](products/equities_funds.md) — **skim leverage**; IC doesn't structure Lombard directly | FI/FX/equity much deeper; leverage lighter |
| D4 | [WM concepts](concepts/wealth_management.md) — **SAA / TAA / DPM mandates, full read**, [CIO view](concepts/cio_house_view.md), [cases × 3](cases/), [alternatives](products/alternatives.md) | Spend more time on the portfolio-construction layer |
| D5 | [Regulation](regulation/) (AI / SFA / PB Code heavy, rather than AML heavy), [**market view — write your own**](market_view.md) [industry](industry.md) | **Writing your own market view** is the IC's rehearsal — practise the one-paragraph-per-asset-class format |
| D6 | **BQ stories — focus on stories 3, 4, 7** (market volatility, difficult client, specialist conflict) + skip business plan | BQ much lighter than RM; **skip business plan entirely** |
| D7 | Mock pass with emphasis on portfolio / product / market questions | Heavy on "how would you construct the SAA…" |

**Non-obvious IC prep priorities:**

1. **A clear, written market view** — 6–8 paragraphs on rates / USD / Asia equities / credit / gold / key risks, with a date. Interviewers will ask you to walk through it.
2. **Able to price / sanity-check a structured-product quote** — for a given ELN, what's the premium component, what IV is implied, is the coupon fair vs what you could get from listed options? You don't need to be a quant; you need to smell bad pricing.
3. **How you work with RMs without undermining them** — story bank item #7 in the BQ/stories set. The classic IC failure mode is making the RM look bad in front of the client; the best ICs elevate the RM while bringing expertise.
4. **Answering "what's your conviction trade right now?"** — expect this. Have two or three specific trades you'd pitch TODAY with a clear thesis, entry, stop, size.

## Common myths about the two roles

**"IC is easier / less pressure than RM."** False in net-compensation terms and in hiring bar. IC is less revenue-volatile but the hiring bar for IC at a top PB is often higher in technical-product depth — the CACS + CFA + deep product fluency barrier is real. RM volatility is high-variance (big years can be very big; bad years can be very bad) but the median RM at a top bank earns more than the median IC at the same tier.

**"You can start as IC and switch to RM."** Occasionally, but not cleanly. RM is a relationship-acquisition path — if you don't build a book of your own relationships early in career, you don't easily pivot sideways into RM at the 5-year mark. The path from IC often goes IC → senior IC → head of IC / regional IC, or IC → CIO / Investment Committee / product specialist leadership.

**"RM is just sales; IC is the 'real' investment role."** Lazy framing. Great RMs are investment-literate and great ICs are commercially savvy. The roles are complementary and the best PB teams treat each other as peers, not hierarchy.

**"Structured-product knowledge is more important for IC."** Yes in mechanics depth; **no** in judgement. RMs have the first conversation with the client where they decide whether a structured product is even appropriate — if the RM doesn't understand the suitability risk, the IC is handed a bad set-up. Both roles need structural literacy; the IC needs a further layer of pricing / Greek / payoff-rigour.

**"Business plans are the decider for RM interviews."** Load-bearing for lateral RM hires with existing books, especially at senior levels (Director+ where a US$500M+ book is expected). For junior RM / ARM transitions and for fresh-graduate programmes, the business plan matters less; the commercial-sense + BQ-story strength matter more.

## How this repo handles the distinction

The repo is **not forked into rm/ and ic/ trees** — that would duplicate ~70% of content and invite drift. Instead:

- **Product pages** are written at IC-depth (payoff math, Greeks intuition, numeric examples) *with* RM-relevant framing (client scenarios, conversation openers, suitability traps). An RM candidate can read the "When to recommend" and "Interview questions" sections and skim the mechanics depth; an IC candidate reads everything.
- **Case studies** ([`cases/`](cases/)) are written in the IC-framing style (SAA / TAA / product-selection reasoning) but include a "client narration" layer for RM prep.
- **BQ stories** ([`bq/stories/`](bq/stories/)) are role-agnostic templates; the competency mapping in `bq/README.md` notes which map best to which role.
- **Business plan** ([`business_plan/template.md`](business_plan/template.md)) is primarily for RM prep. IC candidates can skip it unless the specific bank asks for a coverage / engagement plan.
- **Regulation pages** apply to both roles equally. Interviews drill these symmetrically.

## Decision prompt

**If you are preparing for an RM / Senior RM / Director-level lateral:** spend 60% of study time on commercial / BQ / business plan, 30% on product breadth, 10% on regulation. Drill the business plan until every number is defensible.

**If you are preparing for an IC / Investment Advisor role:** spend 60% on product / portfolio / market, 25% on BQ (focused on specialist / judgement stories), 10% on regulation, 5% on the commercial side (only enough to show you understand how your work connects to revenue).

**If you are preparing for both:** the IC side takes longer to prep, so if splitting time under a 1-week constraint, frontload IC study (Days 1–5) and use Days 6–7 for RM-specific commercial + BQ + business plan work.
