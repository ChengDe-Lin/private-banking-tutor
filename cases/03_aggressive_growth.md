# Case 03: Aggressive Growth, Long Horizon

## Client profile

- **Name (pseudonym):** Mr Kumar
- **Age:** 35
- **Family:** Single, no dependents; parents financially independent; likely family formation in next 5 years
- **Occupation:** Senior VP Engineering at a US-listed tech multinational (long-tenured; moved to SG 5 years ago on regional role)
- **Residence:** SG resident (work pass); considering SG PR eventually
- **Tax:** SG tax-resident; not a US person (Indian national; no US citizenship / green card)
- **Compensation:** Annual cash salary US$350k + annual equity grants (vesting RSUs) ~US$450k/yr (post-tax) that he sells at vest → net cash accretion ~US$250k/yr
- **Net worth:** US$8M liquid + US$1M SG property (co-own with parents) + unvested US equity tail
- **Classification:** AI (opted in 2024); financially-literate early-adopter

## Constraints & objectives

**Primary objective:** **Maximise long-horizon wealth** — 20+ year horizon, no near-term withdrawal requirements. Targets real-return compounding above 5% p.a. net of fees.

**Constraints:**

- **Liquidity:** minimal near-term needs; annual cashflow positive ~US$300k+ continuing; SG lifestyle under US$80k/yr; happily plowing back excess income
- **Risk tolerance:** self-reports "aggressive" — verified by 2020 behaviour (bought the dip) and 2022 (held through drawdown, added tech names); genuinely aggressive
- **Horizon:** 20+ years
- **Interest in alternatives:** yes — actively curious about PE, VC, hedge funds; has personal network giving limited access via angel investing (small check sizes)
- **FX base:** SGD for lifestyle, but comfortable with USD-dominant exposure given his compensation and long US-tech cycle familiarity

**What he explicitly wants:**

- Meaningful PE / VC allocation — sees long-term premium; tolerant of illiquidity
- Thematic exposure to AI / automation / climate — areas he knows from his work
- Tactical flexibility — active satellite where he wants to engage
- Tax-efficient structuring for SG residency (no CGT is his biggest enabler)

**What he explicitly does NOT want:**

- Heavy bond allocation — dislikes "drag on compound growth"
- Structured products sold for yield-enhancement — he'd rather compound equity risk than short puts for coupon
- Legacy-focused planning — no estate concern at 35

**PE / PD capital-call planning — the big operational point for a client at this age.** Total commitments US$1.56M committed over 4–5 years → peak paid-in could be US$1.2M ~2029. He needs to keep US$1.2M of liquid-ready-to-call assets during that period. IG bond allocation (5%, US$400k) plus cash (3%, US$240k) + tactical equity liquidity together exceed the peak call need. **But** — if market drops and he wants to add equity while capital calls are hitting, discipline required: capital calls are contractual, tactical equity is discretionary. Priority goes to calls.

**NOT recommended:**

- **Yield-enhancement structured products (DCI, ELN, FCN, accumulators)** — wrong vehicle for his objective; short-vol bets that trade upside for coupon he doesn't need.
- **Levered ETFs** — tracking error; day-trading instruments.
- **Bond barbell** — oversized bond allocation would drag compound growth.
- **Single-name concentrations in tech** — counter-productive diversification given his compensation exposure.

## Risks to flag

1. **Employer-equity concentration.** His unvested US-listed tech RSUs are US-equity beta on top of his US$3.2M DM equity core. Total US-tech beta is higher than it looks. Mitigation: bias DM core to non-US large-cap tech; sell RSUs at vest (not hold).
2. **PE capital-call cashflow risk.** Defaulting on a call is catastrophic; plan US$1.2M buffer across years 3–5.
3. **Thematic-ETF concentration.** 10% thematic is aggressive; track for style drift; rebalance rigorously.
4. **Drawdown tolerance in practice.** 30–40% portfolio drawdown in a severe scenario is 2008-scale. He says he'd hold; annual behavioural review will test.
5. **Jurisdictional risk.** SG residency currently; career moves could flip tax base (US tax if ever US-person; India tax if repatriation).
6. **Family formation.** In 5 years he likely has a spouse and children; risk profile will shift. Annual reviews to re-assess.
7. **Illiquidity stack.** PE + PD + HF = 27% illiquid or semi-liquid. Cannot unwind quickly if life events force liquidity.
8. **Behavioural alpha erosion.** Active engagement in thematic satellite can morph into active trading, eroding the aggressive-growth compound machine. Pre-agreed tactical budget (max 20% turnover/yr) caps the risk.

## Interview follow-ups

1. **"Why 17% PE? That's more than the traditional 'endowment' 10–15%."** — Three justifications. (a) 20-year horizon supports deeper illiquidity premium capture; (b) his cash flow is genuinely independent of the portfolio (US$250k+ accretion/yr from employment), so he can fund calls without liquidating equity; (c) dispersion in PE is enormous — 17% gives him meaningful position sizing across multiple vintages, whereas 8–10% would be sub-scale. Conservative adjustment would be 12–15%; defensible either way.

2. **"Why not just 100% equity for 20 years?"** — Historically, 100% equity for 20 years does outperform diversified aggressive by some margin. Reasons to diversify: (a) drawdown experience in a bad decade can break even "aggressive" clients; (b) PE and private debt both add non-beta returns (alpha + illiquidity premium) that compound with the equity book; (c) behavioural insurance — a 50% drawdown on 100% equity would shake even him, and a broken rebalancing cycle costs more than the ~50 bps annual drag from 12% non-equity. It's a reasonable debate; 85% equity risk (including PE's equity-like component) is already "mostly equity."

3. **"He wants thematic AI — how do you avoid this being a fad chase?"** — Four controls. (a) Size — 10% cap, rebalancing rule to trim above 12%; (b) vehicle — ETFs and diversified funds, not single-name concentrated bets; (c) review discipline — quarterly re-evaluate whether the thesis has played out or evolved; (d) sell-discipline — if the area rallies 50%+ in a year, rebalance-to-target. The thematic sleeve pays a signal / engagement value (keeps him engaged with the portfolio, brings his AI-sector insight in); it has to be bounded to avoid becoming the tail that wags the dog.

4. **"What if his company IPOs or gets acquired and he gets a big equity event?"** — Any liquidity event would significantly change his profile. If US$5M+ flows into the portfolio from an equity event: rebalance the entire SAA; increase diversification intensity; lower employer-equity beta; potentially add a family-formation-focused liquidity bucket. If he has unvested RSUs being accelerated, evaluate hold-vs-sell per position. And — importantly — if the tax treatment changes (e.g. share-sale via US entity creating US-person tax issues), get specialist tax advice early, not post-event.

5. **"Is he actually 'Aggressive' or just 'Growth'?"** — The four-triangulation test (stated / prior / capacity / psychology) clears him on all four: says aggressive, behaved aggressive in 2020 + 2022, has capacity (income stream independent of portfolio), seems psychologically steady (no phone calls on bad days). Defensible to profile Aggressive. Will re-verify at the 12-month review; if capacity weakens (job loss, family formation, illness), step down to Growth.
