# Case 01: HNW Income Seeker

## Client profile

- **Name (pseudonym):** Mr Tan
- **Age:** 62
- **Family:** Married, spouse age 60, two adult children (34 and 30) financially independent, one grandchild
- **Occupation:** Recently retired, ex-CFO of a SG-listed industrials group, 28-year career
- **Residence and tax status:** Singapore resident and tax-resident
- **Net worth:** ~SGD 28M equivalent, of which:
  - US$20M liquid in USD deposit (subject of this engagement — cleaned out from brokerage at previous bank)
  - SGD 5M SG property (primary residence, no mortgage)
  - SGD 3M SG property (investment, rental yield ~3%)
  - SGD 500k CPF Ordinary / SA accounts
  - SGD 200k cash / insurance policies
- **Base currency for lifestyle:** SGD (lives in SG); holds US$20M in USD for asset-growth / carry reasons
- **Classification:** Accredited Investor (opted in 2022), not a US person

## Constraints & objectives

**Primary objective:** Generate steady annualised **4.5–5.5% income** on the US$20M liquid pool — approximately US$900k–US$1.1M per year — to fund lifestyle (SGD 350k/yr spending) while preserving real capital for legacy to children.

**Constraints:**

- **Liquidity:** wants annual withdrawal capacity (US$500–800k/yr net of re-investment of surplus) with flexibility for one-off needs (medical, property, gifts to children).
- **Risk tolerance:** self-reports "moderate" — confirmed via 2022 questionnaire. Practical check: sat through 2020 COVID drawdown without selling; trimmed in 2022 but did not panic. Appears genuinely moderate.
- **FX:** SGD lifestyle base, but wants to retain USD exposure as a long-run inflation / carry hedge against SGD-only wealth. Willing to convert ~US$200k/yr to SGD for spending.
- **Horizon:** ~20-year horizon (age 62 → 82 personal; longer for legacy).
- **Tax:** SG personal tax exemption on offshore income; no CGT on investments; no estate tax — key base-case assumption.
- **No US tax complications** — no green card, no children in US jobs requiring FATCA planning.
- **Preferences (soft):** values understanding of positions; uncomfortable with products he can't explain; prefers transparency on fees; had negative experience with a 2008-era structured note (knock-in event caused stress) — wants limited structured-product exposure.

**What he explicitly does NOT want:**

- Active trading — not interested in being active portfolio participant.
- High-yield / junk bond concentration — references headlines about China property defaults.
- Single-stock concentration — saw peers hurt with concentration risk in 2008.

**Total: 100% / US$20M.**

**Expected blended portfolio yield / return:** ~4.5–5.0% current yield, with 2–3% capital-appreciation tail (total-return target ~6–7% annualised over 5-year horizon, though this is *expected*, not contractual). Income withdrawal budget: 4.5–5.0%, leaving 1–2% buffer for compounding.

### Tactical Asset Allocation (TAA) — current tilts (as of 2026-04-17)

- Small **underweight** equity (by 2%) — cycle position concerns.
- Small **overweight** USD IG (by 2%) — attractive yield still.
- **Neutral** on duration (rate-cut timing uncertain).
- **Underweight** EM / Asia-ex-JP (given Mr Tan's moderate profile) — would be higher for more aggressive client.

### Mandate structure

**Recommended: DPM (Discretionary) mandate on 70% + Advisory satellite on 30%.**

- **DPM** (core bond ladder, equity ETF core, IG allocation, REITs, alt sleeve) — low-turnover, professional management, flat 75 bps fee on that portion; bank's incentive aligned on AUM growth. Removes trailer-fee conflict on fund choices.
- **Advisory** (30% satellite) — tactical positions, specific bond-issue preferences he wants input on, the gold / alternatives sleeve he wants to understand trade-by-trade. Per-transaction fee model.
- Reviewed quarterly; annual re-balance + deep review.

## Risks to flag to the client

1. **Rate risk.** A +150 bp parallel shift would mark the bond core down ~7–10% temporarily. Coupons keep flowing; recovery is 2–3 years of coupon. **Mitigation**: laddering, duration budget ≤ 5 years, T-bill sleeve as dry powder.
2. **Credit risk.** One in 25 IG issuers could deteriorate — historical through-the-cycle IG defaults ~0.3% p.a., rising 2–3× in recession. Max single-name exposure ~4% of bond sleeve. Diversification reduces but doesn't eliminate.
3. **S-REIT drawdown risk.** 2022 SG REIT index -20%; clients tolerate if income continues (distribution cuts possible). 5% sleeve sized so max drawdown is 1% of portfolio.
4. **Alternatives illiquidity.** 10% sleeve cannot be redeemed in a week. Comfortable because Mr Tan has 10% cash + T-bills + IG ladder liquidity on the other side.
5. **FX risk on USD → SGD withdrawals.** Annual ~US$200k conversion means year-to-year variance in SGD terms of +/- 10%. **Mitigation**: rolling 12M forward hedge on planned annual withdrawals (see [`../products/fx.md`](../products/fx.md)).
6. **Inflation risk.** SGD spending growth ~3% p.a. vs. portfolio nominal return ~6%. Real return ~3% — real legacy preservation achievable but not guaranteed. Would revisit with TIPS overlay if inflation expectations shift materially.
7. **Concentration risk in the DPM mandate.** Mr Tan relinquishes trade-level control. **Mitigation**: clear investment policy statement, quarterly review, annual deep review, ability to opt out of DPM with 30-day notice.
8. **Behavioural risk in next drawdown.** He reports moderate tolerance; the real test is the next 20–30% equity drawdown. Pre-agreed rebalancing rules (don't sell into drawdowns) and conversation now, not during the drawdown, reduce behavioural risk.

## Interview follow-ups

1. **"Why DPM on core and advisory on satellite rather than 100% DPM or 100% advisory?"** — The core is low-turnover, high-conviction, professionally-managed exposure that genuinely benefits from discretion (bond ladder rebalancing, passive core ETFs, trailer-fee elimination). The satellite is where Mr Tan has specific views / preferences he wants expressed with per-trade control (specific bond issues, tactical REITs, gold). The hybrid matches his actual engagement — he's not active-active, but he's not fully delegating either. Cost-effective (he gets flat DPM fee on 70%, per-transaction on 30%).

2. **"What would you change if his wife becomes his successor next year (he dies early)?"** — First, confirm current estate structure (wills, power of attorney, joint account arrangements). SG has no estate tax, so no tax-planning overhead; but jurisdictional complication if assets outside SG. Shift risk tolerance marginally more conservative given the widow's lower investment experience. Increase structured fixed-income component; simplify the alt sleeve. Annual review rather than quarterly. Second, engage his wife in the relationship now — joint annual reviews, introduce the product IC who'd handle continuity. This is standard "RM continuity planning" work.

3. **"Why not pitch him a 6% autocallable that would yield higher?"** — Three reasons. (a) His 2008 structured-product experience tells me his psychological tolerance for knock-in events is low; mechanically suitable does not equal practically suitable. (b) The 6% headline coupon on an autocallable is a short-put plus knock-out structure; the extra 1.5% above IG bonds is pure risk premium he doesn't need to earn given his lifestyle cashflow requirement is already met. (c) The PB Code's "reasonable basis" obligation doesn't mean "push the highest-fee product the client could theoretically tolerate"; it means product fit to objective. Autocallable fails this for income-with-preservation.

4. **"Is the 10% alternatives allocation too high for moderate?"** — Defensible either way. Within the 10%, I've chosen specifically low-vol strategies (multi-strat HF, PE secondaries) rather than blow-up-risk alternatives. Total-portfolio drawdown-contribution from the 10% is small even in a bad scenario. A more conservative alternative would be 5% HF + 5% additional IG bonds, giving up ~30–50 bps of expected return for lower illiquidity. Would discuss with Mr Tan; his moderate profile supports 10% with the right composition, but I'd accept 5% if that's his preference.

5. **"What's the first thing you'd do after onboarding completes?"** — Schedule the 30-day follow-up conversation. The first month's activity will include the bond ladder build, the DPM mandate kickoff, and the initial equity and REIT ETF purchases. At day 30 we'd review: what was bought, what the initial marks look like, whether Mr Tan has questions about anything he's seeing on statements, and any adjustments needed. Then quarterly rhythm established. Building trust in the first 90 days is what makes the 20-year relationship work.
