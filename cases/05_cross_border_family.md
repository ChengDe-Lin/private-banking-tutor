# Case 05: Cross-Border Taiwanese Family

## Client profile

- **Name (pseudonym):** Mr and Mrs Wu, + father-in-law Mr Huang (the family patriarch holding most of the wealth)
- **Ages:** Mr Wu 52, Mrs Wu 50, Mr Huang 78, three children: son 22 (MIT undergrad, plans US career), daughter 19 (Cambridge economics), son 16 (still in Taipei high school)
- **Occupation:** Mr Wu is CEO of a TW-listed family manufacturing company; Mrs Wu runs a boutique investment office for the family; Mr Huang is retired founder of the manufacturing business
- **Residence and tax status:** All three adults TW tax-resident; son in US on F-1 visa not yet substantial-presence; daughter UK student visa non-dom
- **Net worth structure (family total):** ~US$180M equivalent, of which:
  - US$80M: listed TW company shareholding (Mr Huang holds majority; gradual intergenerational transfer ongoing)
  - US$60M: diversified portfolio currently at TW domestic PB + HK PB (legacy structures)
  - US$25M: HK / TW real estate
  - US$15M: private-business interests and family operating assets

**Cross-border dimensions — the complexity engine:**

- **Taiwan:** home base; TW tax rules; NTD-local; potential TW estate tax implications on Mr Huang
- **Hong Kong:** legacy PB relationship since 1990s; HKD / USD booking; portion of family liquid assets
- **Singapore:** proposed new booking centre for diversification portfolio; MAS 13O family office structure considered
- **US:** son's education → work track; US-person risk on the son over time; FATCA implications
- **UK:** daughter currently; non-domicile regime if she stays long-term
- **China:** family business has China-manufacturing operations; commercial exposure to cross-strait dynamics

**Classification:** AI eligible (all parties); family-office structure in consideration

## Constraints & objectives

**Primary objective:** **Consolidate and simplify the family's wealth structure for resilience across jurisdictions, succession, and next-generation governance** — moving away from the ad-hoc multi-PB / multi-jurisdiction sprawl that accumulated over 30 years.

**Secondary objectives:**

- **Intergenerational transition** — Mr Huang's wealth transfer to son (Mr Wu) and eventually grandchildren; minimise friction and family conflict
- **Operational business protection** — ring-fence family liquid assets from the operating business; enable liquidity events (share sales, diversification) without family disruption
- **Cross-border compliance** — FATCA / CRS / regional tax treatment consistent; avoid future surprises
- **Children's future optionality** — each child may end up resident in a different country; structure should support that

**Constraints:**

- **Liquidity:** Mr Huang has ~US$2M/yr lifestyle + medical; Mr and Mrs Wu ~US$1M/yr; total US$3M/yr withdrawal need from portfolios
- **Risk tolerance:** Mr Huang conservative (capital preservation); Mr Wu balanced (growth with preservation); Mrs Wu (running the family investment office) balanced-to-growth
- **Cross-strait sensitivity** — family business has China revenue; cross-border tensions could affect valuation and operations; family wants to diversify *away* from pure TW risk
- **Chinese-language preference** — family communication is Mandarin / Minnan; wealth-management communications must be bi-lingual; documents where possible in TC
- **Trust-based relationships** — Mr Huang especially values personal relationships over platforms; team continuity matters

**What they explicitly want:**

- Gradual transition — not a big-bang restructure
- SG booking as *diversification*, not replacement — maintain TW and HK relationships
- Next-generation involvement — bring daughter and eldest son into meetings progressively
- Education funding ring-fenced — US$500k/yr for each of the two overseas children as long as in school

**What they explicitly do NOT want:**

- Liquidating the TW family business for diversification — not on the table
- Aggressive US-person planning — son hasn't triggered US-person status yet, don't pre-empt
- Complex trust structures that family governance cannot manage

## Recommended approach

### Structure: Gradual 3-phase transition

**Phase 1 (Year 1) — Establish SG booking, consolidate HK positions:**

- Open SG PB account in name of a new holding vehicle (SG VCC or similar) — beneficiary is the family
- Migrate **US$30M** from HK PB to SG PB (diversification sleeve) — keep US$30M at HK as transitional
- Clean documentation trail — SOW on family business wealth, SOF on each migration, FATCA + CRS across all jurisdictions
- **Family office option:** consider MAS 13O tax incentive if committing US$10M+ in designated investments + local hiring (modest)

**Phase 2 (Year 2) — Move portion of TW PB-held assets to SG:**

- After Phase 1 proves trusted, migrate another US$20M from TW domestic PB to SG (further diversification)
- Establish SG-law family trust for generational-transfer assets (eventually holding some TW company shares once Mr Huang transfers them)
- Children's education-funded accounts established in each's residency jurisdiction

**Phase 3 (Year 3-5) — Generational transfer:**

- Mr Huang gradually transfers TW company shareholding to Mr Wu via gift / family-deed mechanism
- Portion of Mr Huang's wealth placed in generation-skipping trust for grandchildren
- Consider PPLI (Private Placement Life Insurance) for cross-border efficiency if US-person risk materialises for son
- Daughter's situation re-evaluated based on where she settles (UK resident-non-dom vs. returning to TW)

### Target SAA for the SG-booked US$50M diversified portfolio (end state Phase 2)

Balanced-Growth profile (blending Mr Huang's caution and Mr Wu's balanced preference):

| Asset class | Target |
|---|---|
| Cash + T-bills | 5% |
| USD IG fixed income | 25% |
| SGD / Asia IG | 10% |
| Global equity (DM + EM) | 30% |
| Alternatives (HF + PE) | 20% |
| Private real-estate (SG / global) | 7% |

### Cross-border structural considerations

**MAS 13O family office option (Phase 1):**

- Commit at least US$10M to designated investments; hire 2+ professionals; minimum local spending
- In exchange, tax exemption on specified income from designated investments
- Operational discipline needed: quarterly reporting, compliance certifications, proper corporate governance
- Recommend only if family is serious about SG as a long-term platform (signals: high-conviction on SG; Mr Wu or daughter's involvement in the office)

**SG-law family trust (Phase 2):**

- Settlor: Mr Huang (with Mr Wu as co-settlor if desired)
- Trustee: professional SG trustee or bank-trustee arm
- Beneficiaries: currently Mr Wu, Mrs Wu, children; potentially grandchildren as remainder
- Purpose: multi-generational wealth, protection from single-jurisdiction risk, family governance
- Needs legal specialist (not bank counsel) for drafting

**FATCA management for son:**

- Currently F-1, non-US person
- If graduates and starts H-1B → US person within a year
- Monitor; at transition point, decide: (a) fork his share into US-compliant structure, (b) have him renounce any future US-person trajectory (not practical if he's building career there), (c) accept the FATCA overhead
- Start conversation early, not post-event

**UK for daughter:**

- Currently student; non-domicile rules may apply if she stays
- Watch 2024 UK non-dom regime changes (regime being phased toward residence-based)
- Decision point: where does she settle post-graduation

### Product picks (high-level for SG-booked portfolio)

- Core DPM mandate on US$30M initial
- Advisory satellite on US$10M for tactical / engagement
- Alternatives: US$10M committed over 3 years across PE + HF + private debt
- Gold + RE sleeve: direct physical gold + SG private RE fund

## Risks to flag

1. **Cross-strait geopolitical risk.** Family business has material China exposure. Potential 2025–2030 geopolitical stress scenarios could hit valuation. Diversification to SG portfolio is partially an explicit hedge against this; portfolio construction should also be low-correlated (avoid concentrated TW / China equity in the SG book).
2. **Intergenerational transition complexity.** Transferring TW company shares is taxable in TW (gift / estate tax); timing and sequencing matter. Require TW tax advisory.
3. **US-person risk for son.** As his career develops US trajectory looks likely; prepare structure options in advance.
4. **UK regime changes for daughter.** Non-dom tax rules under review; monitor.
5. **Mr Huang's capacity and mortality risk.** At 78, capacity + succession planning urgency increases. Advance directives, power of attorney, successor trustee arrangements should be in place Year 1.
6. **Family governance friction.** Three adult decision-makers with different preferences; one aging patriarch; need structured family-governance process (annual family meetings, decision rights, dispute resolution).
7. **SG regulatory changes.** 13O and VCC framework have evolved since 2020; may tighten further. Track.
8. **Compliance risk during migration.** Moving US$50M cross-border across multiple banks triggers CDD re-verification at each; ensure clean paperwork, avoid hitting red flags.
9. **Operating-business / personal-wealth mixing.** Keep family-business assets separate from personal wealth — clear legal ring-fencing.
10. **Banker continuity.** Family values relationships — sudden RM change mid-transition could derail. Team-based coverage, senior backup.

## Interview follow-ups

1. **"Why SG as the diversification jurisdiction — not Switzerland or Dubai?"** — Five reasons for this family. (a) Language / cultural fit — SG has Mandarin-speaking bankers, cultural familiarity for TW families; (b) geography — SG-TW flights are short, family visits are practical; (c) MAS 13O regime — tax efficiency for family office structures, which Swiss / Middle-East alternatives don't uniquely offer; (d) political stability and rule of law — Mr Huang values regulatory predictability; (e) inter-generational positioning — SG is a natural hub for the next generation's multi-jurisdictional lifestyle. Switzerland works for wealth but has less Asia-native infrastructure for this family's needs. Dubai has similar political-stability story but less developed family-office-specific framework.

2. **"The son is about to trigger US-person status — what do you do?"** — Three-step approach. (a) Educate the family NOW — before he becomes a US person — on what FATCA means for him, his future account structures, and the compliance overhead. (b) Decide governance: will he inherit TW assets? Will his share be structured separately? Will he be excluded from family-pool decisions to avoid FATCA-reporting burden on the whole? (c) If he becomes US-person and is an equal heir, structure his share in a US-compliant wrapper — PPLI is a strong option; US-compliant mutual-fund structure another; avoid PFIC exposure in his account specifically. Work with specialist US-tax counsel (not bank counsel). Cost of inaction: if the family structure gets him as a beneficiary without planning, the entire trust structure can be caught in FATCA complexities. Mitigation needs 12–18 months of runway, not last-minute.

3. **"How do you handle a patriarch who wants final say on everything?"** — Respect the cultural expectation. Mr Huang's generation expects final authority; it's not about decision-quality — it's about respect and legitimacy. Three tactical moves. (a) Design the process so Mr Huang chairs the family meetings; decisions are presented to him for formal ratification, even if the technical substance was decided in Mr Wu + Mrs Wu + advisors meetings beforehand. (b) Respect the speed he sets — if he wants to think for 3 months before a major decision, 3 months. (c) As capacity declines, gradually delegate via power of attorney and successor-trustee arrangements while maintaining honorific role. This is common across Asian family-business clients; the cultural navigation is central to the relationship.

4. **"What's the MAS 13O framework and why pitch it to this family?"** — MAS Section 13O is a tax incentive for family-office / fund-manager structures managed from Singapore. Requirements (as at 2024–2025, verify currency): commit at least S$20M in designated investments; employ at least 2 investment professionals; spend at least S$200k/yr on local expenses; meet other governance and reporting criteria. In exchange: tax exemption on specified income (dividends, interest, gains) from designated investments. For this family, the benefit: (a) US$10M+ anyway in SG portfolio; (b) spousal team can formally employ their investment oversight; (c) institutional credibility for next-generation succession; (d) SG-pegging as a long-term family platform. Downsides: ongoing compliance + reporting; governance overhead; limits operational flexibility. Right for serious long-term SG commitment; wrong if this is just a one-off diversification.

5. **"What's the biggest risk in the plan — and how do you mitigate?"** — The biggest single risk is that the family relationship ruptures mid-transition — e.g. a TW tax audit triggered by the cross-border migrations; or Mr Huang's health declines mid-process; or a geopolitical event damages the TW business valuation and triggers emergency restructuring. All three would derail the measured Phase-1–3 plan. Mitigation: (a) start with documentation and relationship-building *before* migrations — 3 months of onboarding and compliance setup before moving money; (b) stage migrations so each is independently defensible — if Phase 2 becomes impossible, Phase 1 still works; (c) senior-banker continuity — a backup RM identified from Day 1; (d) specialist advisory — tax counsel TW + SG, trust counsel SG, family-governance advisory — to catch issues the PB bank wouldn't see. Cost: transition completes in 3–5 years not 18 months. Value: robust durable family wealth platform.
