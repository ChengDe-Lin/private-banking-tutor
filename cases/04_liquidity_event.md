# Case 04: Post-Exit Liquidity Event

## Client profile

- **Name (pseudonym):** Mr Lee
- **Age:** 42
- **Family:** Married (spouse 40, previously CMO of same company, now exploring philanthropy), two children ages 10 and 7
- **Occupation:** Co-founder of a consumer-tech company sold to a US strategic in late 2025 for ~US$280M (pre-tax); his share after founder dilution and carve-outs: **US$55M gross → US$50M net after advisory / banker / escrow / clawback reserves**
- **Prior PB relationship:** None; banked previously with retail accounts and company's corporate banker
- **Residence and tax status:** Singapore resident (move from US 3 years ago for tax / family reasons); SG tax-resident
- **Classification:** AI (to be opted in during onboarding)

## Constraints & objectives

**Primary objective:** **Convert a sudden US$50M liquidity event into a durable long-term wealth plan** — this is his once-in-a-lifetime wealth moment. Wants to avoid the "big lottery winner" mistakes: no rapid deployment, no emotional buys, careful integration with life decisions still to be made.

**Secondary objectives:**

- **Family stability** — ensure lifestyle + children's education + legacy are secured before any "interesting" allocation
- **Next chapter** — probably angel investing + possible second startup; wants liquidity for that
- **Estate planning** — first serious engagement; currently has rudimentary wills, no trusts
- **Philanthropy** — spouse wants to direct ~US$5M over 5 years to focused causes (education, mental health)

**Constraints:**

- **Liquidity:** Needs US$500k–1M/yr for lifestyle + children's international-school fees (US$120k/yr for 2 kids); US$2M reserved for a possible property purchase in next 18 months; flexibility for potential second-startup capital
- **Risk tolerance:** His psychology is high-capacity but *scarred by the recent exit experience* — knows how fragile company valuations are, hesitant to put the whole US$50M immediately into risk assets
- **Horizon:** 40+ years personal; multi-generational
- **Tax:** SG no CGT on the exit; US W-8BEN (not a US person); FATCA/CRS done
- **Emotional:** he's exhausted from the exit process; wants structure and a plan rather than being pitched 20 products

**What he explicitly does NOT want:**

- Rushed deployment — "I just did the big thing, I don't need to do another big thing tomorrow"
- Structured products sold for yield — doesn't understand them, unwilling to trust anything he can't explain back
- 100% discretionary — wants to retain advisory engagement on major allocations

## Recommended allocation

### Phased deployment over 18 months

**Principle:** Don't deploy US$50M at a single point — sequence the deployment to reduce timing risk, allow learning, and match real decisions as life unfolds.

**Month 0–3 (Initial setup):**

| Bucket | Amount | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Operating reserve | US$3M | T-bills / SGD money market — 2 years of lifestyle + education + buffer |
| Goal-matched property reserve | US$2M | Short-dated USD IG + cash — liability-matched for property purchase window |
| Philanthropy commitment reserve | US$5M | USD IG + cash — ring-fenced for spouse's 5-year giving plan |
| Initial diversified core (DPM mandate) | US$20M | Launch the long-term core |
| Alternatives commitment reserve | US$7M | Hold as T-bills; commit PE/PD/HF over 18–24 months |
| Tactical satellite / growth | US$10M | Advisory — global equity, thematic, small single-name, start over 12 months |
| Strategic cash / angel investment | US$3M | Reserved for his possible second-startup or angel investing over next 5 years |

Total = US$50M, with US$10M in operational/goal-matched cash-like, US$20M in long-term core, US$17M in growth / alt commitments pending deployment, US$3M in strategic cash.

- **Advisory satellite US$10M** — per-trade engagement; client engagement on views
- **Alternatives** — primary fund commitments (US$7M over 18–24 months); separate documentation

### Capital calls

US$7M committed to alts → peak paid-in US$5M over years 3–5. US$10M reserve sleeve (operating + property + philanthropy) covers this. PE capital-call planning is the key operational note.

### Estate / structure pieces to address in Month 1–6

- **Wills and incapacity planning** — updated both his and spouse's wills; lasting power of attorney; medical directive.
- **Trust structures** — consider a SG-law family trust for long-term family wealth; consult specialist lawyer.
- **Life insurance** — his health, age 42, Mr Lee is uninsured; given US$50M liquidity he may not need large coverage, but review.
- **Pre-nuptial / spousal protocols** — not raised; generally assumed marriage-stable.
- **Children's education planning** — dedicated vehicle (529-equivalent / SG-side investment account) in each child's name for university funding.
- **Philanthropy structure** — donor-advised fund or private foundation consideration for tax-efficiency + governance; MAS / IRAS treatment.

## Risks to flag

1. **Timing-risk in initial deployment.** Deploying US$20M core over 6 months; dollar-cost-averaging the deployment smooths but extends timing risk. Phased deployment (25% month 1, 25% month 2, 25% month 3, 25% month 6) mitigates.
2. **Over-deployment risk.** Pressure (internal or from bank) to deploy all US$50M fast. Discipline: pace matches goals, not bank AUM-targets.
3. **Philanthropy governance.** US$5M to his spouse's direction; need agreed criteria, decision-making cadence, family communication. Risk: poorly-targeted giving.
4. **Family-office transition risk.** As wealth builds post-exit, a family-office structure may become appropriate. Timing (maybe 5-year horizon) and scale (US$100M+ typical) — review periodically.
5. **Post-exit identity risk.** Mr Lee may struggle with "what next." Bank's role is financial; he and spouse benefit from external coaching / peer network. (Suggest, don't impose.)
6. **Aggressive "next startup" risk.** US$3M strategic cash may not be enough if he pivots; keep option for him to re-deploy from other buckets but don't plan the portfolio around speculative startup paths.
7. **Sector-concentration blind spot.** His recent exit was in consumer-tech; he has implicit knowledge advantage there, may over-invest in that sector. Diversification discipline.
8. **SG residency-risk.** If he relocates (back to US for children, or elsewhere), tax and regulatory profile changes. Annual re-assess.

## Interview follow-ups

1. **"Why phase deployment rather than deploying all US$50M in month 1?"** — Three reasons. (a) Timing risk — markets in month 1 vs 12 vs 18 are materially different; averaging-in smooths; (b) client-experience — Mr Lee has decision fatigue from the exit; spreading decisions gives him space to engage with each allocation rather than rubber-stamp everything; (c) life-integration — his family plans (property, possible second startup, philanthropy rollout) will sharpen over 6–12 months; the portfolio should respond to that, not precede it. Cost: potential upside missed if markets rally during deployment window — accept that, but mitigated by phased approach. The psychological cost of a 30% drawdown in month 2 on US$50M deployed at peak is higher than the financial cost of missing 5% rally during phased entry.

2. **"He says no structured products — is that final?"** — For now, yes. His reasoning is "I can't explain the risk." That's actually the PB Code suitability posture — if he can't explain the mechanism and payoff, he shouldn't hold the product. In 2–3 years, if trust builds and he's engaged in IC discussions on product mechanics, I'd consider a small principal-protected sleeve if specifically fitting. I wouldn't push in the first 24 months.

3. **"How do you handle the US$5M philanthropy allocation?"** — Three structural options: (a) direct giving account — simplest, spouse writes cheques; (b) donor-advised fund (DAF) — tax-efficient where applicable, professional grant admin, flexibility over time; (c) private foundation — most governance, highest admin overhead, suitable for larger multi-generational charitable intent. At US$5M and 5-year giving horizon, a DAF is usually the right instrument — combines tax-efficiency with donor autonomy without the foundation overhead. The portfolio manages the US$5M in IG + cash until each grant is made.

4. **"Spouse wants equal input — how do you manage joint advisory?"** — Standard best-practice for couple relationships at this scale: both named on account, both included in quarterly reviews, both sign on major allocation changes. For DPM, one signatory is sufficient operationally but spouse receives same reporting. I'd run the onboarding meeting with both present; establish that both have information-symmetry; identify where decisions split (e.g. spouse may lead on philanthropy; he leads on growth/tactical). PB Code confidentiality allows this with joint-account structure.

5. **"What's the biggest mistake he could make in the first year?"** — Not phased deployment — the biggest single-decision risk is an impulsive big bet. "I just did a company; I can do another." A US$10M single-bet "second startup" investment with insufficient diligence, driven by momentum from the exit, could unwind US$10M in 12 months. The portfolio plan reserves US$3M for that bucket explicitly to cap the damage. My role is to flag the behavioural risk without being patronising; his spouse's role is often more valuable here than mine.
