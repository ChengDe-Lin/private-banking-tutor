# Case 02: Concentrated Single-Stock Exposure

## Client profile

- **Name (pseudonym):** Ms Chen
- **Age:** 48
- **Family:** Married (spouse 50, private-sector professional SGD 400k income), two children ages 12 and 10
- **Occupation:** Co-founder and current Chairperson of a HK-listed technology company ("TechCo"), founded 2005, IPO 2014
- **Residence:** Hong Kong resident; considering SG relocation for family / education reasons
- **Net worth structure:** ~US$45M equivalent, of which:
  - **US$30M in TechCo shares** (HK-listed, ~12% of her gross net worth concentrated in one name, 70% of her household net worth)
  - US$8M investment portfolio at another PB (IG bonds + public equity)
  - US$5M HK residential property (primary residence)
  - US$2M cash / HKD deposits
- **TechCo share cost basis:** essentially zero (founder equity, pre-IPO). HK has no capital gains tax, so outright sale is not tax-constrained — but sending a sell signal publicly would be dilutive and potentially disruptive.
- **Classification:** AI (both HK status and SG where applicable), not a US person

## Constraints & objectives

**Primary objective:** **Diversify the 70% single-name concentration without alarming the market or signaling strategic exit** from TechCo. Wants to reduce TechCo exposure from 70% of household net worth to ~25–30% over 2–3 years.

**Secondary objectives:**

- Maintain Chairperson role with adequate remaining shareholding to exercise control (current stake: 12% of company; wants to keep above 8%).
- Diversify proceeds into a global portfolio consistent with long-term wealth growth for her children's legacies.
- Explore SG booking in parallel with HK for the diversification portfolio.

**Constraints:**

- **Disclosure rules:** HK Securities and Futures Ordinance requires disclosure of substantial-shareholder dealings above certain thresholds (notifiable interests, Part XV disclosures). Large block sales would be disclosed and market-moving.
- **Insider / blackout periods:** as a director, blackout windows pre-earnings, pre-material-announcements restrict trading windows.
- **Market-timing:** TechCo is at all-time highs; she's aware concentration resolution is prudent but also aware any sale forgoes upside.
- **Family dynamics:** husband supports diversification; children are aware of TechCo (father of friends asks them about it). Wants to keep family conversation calm.
- **Risk tolerance:** practical tolerance is high — she built a company through 2008 and COVID — but concentration scares her; moderate after diversification, more risk-accepting on the diversified portion.

**What she explicitly does NOT want:**

- Selling shares in a visible public block that signals Chairperson losing confidence.
- Derivative structures that would be perceived as "founder hedging" before a crisis.
- Tax / estate complications — wants to keep structures simple.

## Recommended allocation

### Phased concentration reduction — 3-year plan

**Phase 1 (Year 1, target reduction: US$10M from TechCo → diversified).**

| Mechanism | Amount | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Disclosed periodic 10b5-1-equivalent plan** (HK equivalent): pre-announced disposal plan sold during non-blackout windows | US$5M (~2M shares) | Transparent to market; removes insider-trading risk; spread over 12 months |
| **Exchange Fund contribution** (contributing shares into a multi-investor fund in exchange for a diversified basket) | US$3M of TechCo shares, received as diversified portfolio | Defers the public-market signal; ~7-year lock-up; US$ 3M minimum |
| **Collar structure** on a further US$5M of TechCo shares (buy 20% OTM put, sell 15% OTM call to fund) | Protects a further 5M for Year 1-2 | Retains some upside to the call strike; removes downside; zero-cost |
| **Prepaid forward** on US$3M: client sells forward 2 years out, receives ~85% cash today | US$3M today, delivering shares in 2 years | Monetises without triggering current-period disposal signal |
| **Do-nothing (held)** | Remaining ~US$14M | Maintains Chairperson shareholding |

**Result:** Year 1 net diversification of ~US$13M (US$5M direct sale + US$3M exchange fund + US$5M hedged via collar); effective net exposure dropped to ~US$17M.

**Phase 2 (Year 2):** Continue disposal plan on another US$8M; re-evaluate exchange-fund and collar roll; assess market conditions.

**Phase 3 (Year 3):** Final US$2–4M disposition; evaluate TechCo residual based on company health and her role; likely maintain ~US$8–10M as Chairperson stake.

### Proceeds allocation (as funds arrive)

**Target SAA for the diversified portfolio** (built up to ~US$20M over 3 years, plus existing US$8M at other PB = US$28M eventual diversified portfolio). Balanced-growth profile given 20-year horizon and children's legacy:

| Asset class | Target weight | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Cash & T-bills | 5% | Liquidity / dry powder |
| USD IG fixed income (3–7Y ladder) | 20% | Income anchor |
| Developed-market equity (ex-TechCo exposure) | 30% | Broad global growth |
| Emerging-market / Asia equity | 10% | Regional exposure; ensure no tech-concentration overlap |
| Private equity (secondaries and direct co-invest via FO platform) | 15% | Long-horizon, illiquidity premium; she has deal flow and knowledge base |
| Hedge funds (multi-strat + equity L/S) | 10% | Absolute-return component |
| Real estate (private RE fund, SG / HK ex-residential) | 5% | Hard-asset diversification |
| Gold | 5% | Tail hedge |

## Product picks with justification

| Slot | Product | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Direct disposal plan | HK-listed disposal plan mechanic with scheduled trading dates | Transparency; compliance; non-signal |
| Exchange fund | A multi-investor exchange fund (7-year lock-up, ~10 underlying high-conviction large-cap names, tax-deferred-equivalent in HK where no CGT) | Diversifies without selling; fees ~1% p.a.; rare but available for qualifying HK UHNW |
| Collar structure | Zero-cost collar on US$5M: long put at 80% of spot (18M), short call at 115% of spot (18M) | Insurance without upfront premium; preserves ~15% upside |
| Prepaid forward | 2-year prepaid forward settled physically in TechCo shares | Monetises future delivery; reduces disposal signal; moderate premium cost |
| Diversified DM equity | MSCI World ETF + selected active manager for developed-value tilt | Low-cost core, diversified broadly away from single-stock Asia tech risk |
| EM equity | MSCI EM ETF; explicit filter to ensure low HK / TechCo-sector overlap | Regional exposure, consciously non-correlated to TechCo's fortune |
| PE (secondaries + direct) | Top-tier secondaries fund + 1–2 direct co-invests in her network | Deal-flow access she has; shorter J-curve via secondaries |
| Multi-strat HF | Citadel / Millennium feeder; small equity-L/S satellite | Low-vol absolute return; diversifier |
| RE | SG / Asia private real-estate fund (non-listed) | Hard-asset; low correlation to TechCo |

**NOT recommended / deliberately excluded:**

- **Selling a large visible block in-market** — market impact and signal costs would be material.
- **Naked short positions on TechCo competitors** — creates perception issues and adds correlated risk.
- **Single-name bets on TechCo competitors** — creates sector overlap, counterproductive.
- **Levered products on the remaining TechCo stake** — would amplify rather than dampen concentration.

## Risks to flag to the client

1. **Exchange-fund 7-year lock-up.** The US$3M is illiquid for 7 years. Acceptable given she has plenty of liquidity elsewhere but flag it.
2. **Collar opportunity cost if TechCo rallies 30%+.** Upside above the 115% call strike foregone. On US$5M, that's US$750k forgone at 30% rally — a real cost. Rebuttal: if she believes TechCo will 2x, don't collar — hold. The collar is for the US$5M she's specifically de-risking.
3. **Disclosure risk.** Even a transparent disposal plan may attract media / analyst attention. **Mitigation**: clear communication with Investor Relations; senior management aligned; story framed as "diversification as founder's responsibility."
4. **Chairperson-role signal risk.** Any reduction below 8% stake could start to signal. Plan caps total sales to maintain above 8%.
5. **Tax mismatch between jurisdictions.** HK has no CGT; SG no CGT; but if she relocates to SG and sells remaining TechCo stakes as SG resident, need to ensure HK exit protocols are observed and no US-person issues (not applicable here).
6. **Counterparty risk on OTC derivatives (collar, prepaid forward).** CSA collateral arrangements; size each structure within prudent counterparty limits.
7. **Behavioural risk — "regret avoidance" if TechCo rallies post-disposal.** Pre-commit to the plan; set up written pre-agreed rebalancing rules; review annually, adjust only on material company / market changes.
8. **Family disclosure risk.** Sharing the plan with spouse / adult children should be managed; children not yet adult but will in 5–10 years; start laying communication groundwork.

## Interview follow-ups

1. **"Why not just sell US$20M outright in a single block?"** — Market impact on a block of that size (~8M shares on a HK mid-cap tech) would be 5–15% price pressure, costing US$1–3M before tax. Signal risk is even larger — analyst community, suppliers, customers would read a Chairperson mega-sale as crisis. The phased approach over 3 years spreads the market impact, smooths the signal, and actually realises a better net outcome. Plus: the collar and exchange-fund pieces diversify without ever hitting the tape.

2. **"Why an exchange fund instead of just selling?"** — Two reasons specific to this profile. (a) Tax-efficiency — in jurisdictions with CGT (which HK isn't — but for US-person clients or if she moves to US, would be material), exchange funds defer the gain. For Ms Chen personally the tax benefit is modest; for US-person family members receiving parts of the diversification, it would matter. (b) Signal-minimisation — contributing shares into a private fund structure is a private transaction; the market doesn't see it as a sale. For a Chairperson who values low-profile diversification, this is often the most valuable mechanism.

3. **"Walk me through the collar mechanics."** — She's long 5M shares of TechCo at spot US$X/share. We buy a 18-month put on those shares at 80% of spot (downside protection), funded by selling an 18-month call at 115% of spot (she gives up upside above 115%). The structure is zero-cost if strikes are symmetric in volatility space; typically needs to be adjusted slightly for her specific tenor and vol levels. Her P&L scenarios over 18 months: (a) stock drops 30% — put delivers, she loses 20% of shares but protected from the incremental 10% drop, effective floor at 80% of entry; (b) stock flat — both options expire worthless; (c) stock rallies to 110% — captures the full 10% upside; (d) stock rallies 30% — capped at 115%, gives up the incremental 15%. Operationally, she retains dividend rights (gets the current dividend flow) and voting rights on those shares — a key advantage of collar vs outright sale.

4. **"She's not a US person — does that change your structuring?"** — Materially, yes. For a US-person client, the exchange-fund mechanic becomes even more valuable (defers US CGT); the collar still works but introduces constructive-sale risk (IRC §1259) that needs careful structuring; and FATCA reporting is relevant through all the new vehicles. Since Ms Chen is not US-person, none of these apply, which simplifies the plan. If her children become US persons (unlikely but possible through marriage / immigration), the portfolio pieces going to them eventually would need to be re-examined.

5. **"What's the single biggest risk to this plan?"** — Paradoxically, the market risk on the 14M+ she's *holding*, not the part she's diversifying. If TechCo drops 40% in the next 18 months, her net worth takes a significant hit on the residual, and the realised diversification is suddenly worth proportionally more (she'd wish she'd done it faster). Hedging the full stake isn't feasible given Chairperson-role constraints. Second-biggest risk: that she changes her mind in Year 2 ("TechCo is having a great year, let me pause") and the plan stalls. Pre-agreed trigger conditions (quarterly review, pre-specified rebalancing rules, not market-timing) are the mitigation.
