# Tax Basics — SG, HK, TW, US, UK for PB Clients

> **PB clients are almost always multi-jurisdictional, and tax is frequently the deciding factor in structuring recommendations.** Candidates don't need to be tax lawyers — but you must know the big-picture tax features of the five jurisdictions that dominate Asia PB conversations (SG, HK, TW, US-reach, UK), and you must recognise when a tax issue needs a specialist versus when it's standard. This page is the orienting layer — not exhaustive tax guidance, which always requires jurisdiction-specific qualified counsel. The rules here are stable in broad direction; specific numbers and regimes **change**, so verify before relying on them for client advice.

## 直覺理解

PB 客戶通常是**多管轄稅務** (multi-jurisdictional tax) 的——居住 SG、持有 TW 資產、孩子在美國、物業在 HK。每個國家對**誰是稅務居民、對什麼課稅、用什麼稅率**都不一樣，而且**兩個國家可能同時主張同一筆收入的課稅權**。專業稅務結構化的核心任務是：**用合法架構 (trust / holdco / insurance wrapper) 讓錢流經最有效率的路徑，並且利用雙邊稅務協定避免雙重課稅**。銀行端的你不做實際的稅務意見——那是 tax lawyer 和 CPA 的工作——但你必須**認得出何時該觸發「我要找稅務專家」的對話**，並且**基本地知道 SG / HK / TW / US / UK 的稅務地圖長什麼樣**。

## The core mental model — three questions per jurisdiction

For any client in any jurisdiction, ask:

1. **Residency** — is the client a tax resident here? How is that determined?
2. **Scope** — if resident, are they taxed on **worldwide** income or only **local-source**?
3. **Tax events** — what triggers tax? Income, capital gains, gifts, inheritance, transactions?

Answering these three for each jurisdiction the client touches gives you the orientation. The structuring follows.

## Singapore

### Residency

- **Tax resident** if physically present in SG for **≥ 183 days** in the year, or on an employment pass.
- Permanent Residents (PR) default to tax resident.

### Scope

- **Territorial system** — SG only taxes **SG-source income** (with some exceptions for employment income received in SG from overseas).
- **Foreign-sourced income** received by an SG-resident individual is **generally not taxed** in SG (there are nuances for certain foreign-sourced income from designated entities — consult specialist).
- This is the main structural reason SG is attractive for UHNW — offshore investment income does not hit SG's tax base.

### Key tax properties for PB clients

| Tax type | SG rate / treatment |
|---|---|
| **Personal income tax** | Progressive 0–24% (up to S$1M income band, 2024–2025 structure) |
| **Capital gains tax** | **None** — no CGT on investment gains for individuals |
| **Dividends received** | Generally tax-exempt (SG corporate one-tier system) |
| **Interest received** | Taxable for SG-source interest; certain foreign-sourced interest exempt |
| **Estate / inheritance tax** | **None** (abolished 2008) |
| **Gift tax** | **None** |
| **Goods & Services Tax (GST)** | Currently **9%** (raised from 7% through 2023-2024) |
| **Property tax** | Progressive based on annual value; ABSD for foreigners on residential |
| **ABSD (Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty)** | Up to **60% for foreign buyers of residential property** (as of April 2023 update — verify current) |

### Investment-adjacent incentives

- **MAS 13O / 13U family-office regime** — tax exemption on specified investment income from designated investments; see [`../industry.md`](../industry.md) and [`estate_planning.md`](estate_planning.md).
- **VCC (Variable Capital Company)** — corporate structure for funds; tax-efficient for private investment vehicles.
- **Pioneer / FSI incentives** — for qualifying companies in specific sectors (less relevant for PB personal tax).

### Why SG works for PB

- No CGT or estate tax — ideal jurisdiction for HNW / UHNW residence for wealth-preservation.
- Strong treaty network — reduces withholding tax on cross-border income.
- Rule of law, political stability, regulatory quality.
- Family-office incentive regime (13O / 13U) post-2020 made SG the APAC destination for FO relocation.

### Common SG tax mistakes clients make

- Assuming all foreign income is completely exempt — specific nuances for employment income received here, for entities with SG PE exposure.
- Over-claiming PR / tax-residency before they've earned it.
- Forgetting property-tax / ABSD cost when diversifying into SG property.
- Missing CRS-reporting obligations because "SG doesn't tax foreign income."

## Hong Kong

### Residency

- HK taxes on **territorial (source)** basis; residency less critical than in other jurisdictions — what matters is **where the income was sourced**.
- For Salaries Tax: income derived from HK employment / services rendered in HK.

### Scope

- **Territorial** — HK only taxes HK-source income.
- No tax on offshore income in most cases.

### Key tax properties

| Tax type | HK rate / treatment |
|---|---|
| **Personal income (salaries) tax** | Progressive 2–17%, capped at 15% standard rate on total income (for total income >threshold) |
| **Capital gains tax** | **None** on capital transactions |
| **Dividends received** | Generally not taxed |
| **Interest received** | HK-sourced interest: exempt in many cases (bank deposit interest exempt for individuals) |
| **Estate duty** | **None** (abolished 2006) |
| **Gift tax** | **None** |
| **Stamp duty on stocks** | 0.13% per side on HK stock trades (2024 revision) — meaningful for active traders |
| **Stamp duty on property** | Buyer's + Ad Valorem + Buyer's Stamp Duty (up to 15% for non-permanent-residents historically, adjusted periodically) |
| **Property tax** | 15% on rental income from HK property |

### Why HK has been attractive for PB (but challenged)

- Similar territorial advantages to SG: no CGT, no estate duty, low income tax.
- Strong banking secrecy historically (now modified by FATCA / CRS).
- Major access point to mainland China wealth.
- Political uncertainty since 2019 has shifted UHNW flow toward SG.

### HK-specific tax structures

- HK companies used as **holdcos** for regional investments — low tax, treaty network.
- **Cross-border structures with mainland China** — historically HK was the default gateway; regime-sensitive.

## Taiwan

### Residency

- Tax resident if: (a) Taiwan-domiciled, OR (b) present in Taiwan for ≥ 183 days in a tax year.
- For non-residents: Taiwan taxes only TW-source income.
- For tax residents: **taxed on worldwide income** (with some exclusions and foreign-tax-credit arrangements).

### Scope

- **Worldwide income** for tax residents (with minimum tax framework for overseas income).
- **Minimum Tax** system: overseas income above TWD 1M per household per year subject to alternative minimum tax; effective rate around **20%** on overseas income above threshold (verify current rules).

### Key tax properties for PB clients (TW residents)

| Tax type | TW rate / treatment |
|---|---|
| **Personal income tax** | Progressive up to 40% |
| **Capital gains on listed securities** | **Exempt** for individuals (since 2016 abolition of securities CGT) — but under minimum-tax rules overseas security gains may be assessed |
| **Capital gains on property (individual)** | Real estate CGT — consolidated "家屋地合一" regime for sales post-2016; rates depend on holding period (up to 45% for short hold on non-self-use) |
| **Dividends** | Progressive inclusion + tax credit system; simplified regime options |
| **Estate tax** | **Yes** — progressive up to **20%** (as of 2024) on worldwide estate for TW residents |
| **Gift tax** | **Yes** — up to **20%** with annual exemption (TWD 2.44M in 2024) |
| **Overseas income minimum tax** | For amounts above TWD 1M/yr from overseas — alternative minimum tax calculation |

### Implications for TW-resident PB clients

- **TW residents face tax on their worldwide income** — this is what drives offshoring of TW wealth to SG / HK.
- **Estate tax of 20% is meaningful** — creates strong motivation for lifetime gifting, trust structures, and offshore-asset positioning with careful execution.
- **CRS participation** — Taiwan is NOT currently a CRS participant, which historically meant SG / HK / other-jurisdiction account information wasn't automatically reported to TW tax authority. Regime-sensitive; verify current status.
- **Cross-strait tax** — TW-China tax relationship is complex and political; CBC restrictions on outbound remittances (annual US$5M individual quota).

### Why TW-origin wealth often relocates to SG / HK

- Worldwide taxation + estate tax of 20% creates strong incentive to structure offshore.
- SG residency (without full TW renunciation — dual-residence complications) offers tax efficiency.
- TW family businesses often held via SG / HK holdcos for tax and structural reasons.

## United States — The Extraterritorial Reach

### Residency / citizenship

- **US citizens and green-card holders are taxed on worldwide income regardless of where they live.** This is the key extraterritorial feature unique to the US.
- Non-US-persons taxed only on US-source income + US-situs asset transfers.

### Scope — for US persons

- **Worldwide income** — every dollar of interest, dividend, rental, capital gain globally is on the US tax return.
- **FBAR** — foreign bank account reporting; aggregate US$10k+ threshold.
- **Form 8938 (FATCA)** — foreign financial asset reporting.
- **PFIC (Passive Foreign Investment Company) rules** — punitive taxation on non-US mutual funds / PFICs.
- **Controlled Foreign Corporation (CFC) rules** — anti-deferral rules on foreign companies.
- **GILTI** — Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income — inclusion of certain CFC income.

### Key US tax properties

| Tax type | US rate / treatment |
|---|---|
| **Federal income tax** | Up to 37% on ordinary income |
| **Long-term capital gains** | 0% / 15% / 20% based on bracket (+ 3.8% NIIT) |
| **Estate tax (federal)** | Up to **40%** on estate above lifetime exemption (US$13.6M for single, 2024 — sunsetting back to ~$5M+ inflation-adjusted in 2026) |
| **Gift tax** | Up to 40%; annual exclusion (US$18k in 2024); lifetime exemption shared with estate |
| **Non-US-person estate tax on US-situs assets** | Up to 40%; **US$60k exemption only** — tiny compared to US-person exemption |
| **State income tax** | 0–13.3% (varies by state); some states no income tax (TX, FL, WA, NV) |

### Implications for Asian PB clients with US connections

- **US-person status can trigger unexpectedly** — children going to US for school, marriage to US person, extended business presence.
- **US-situs assets** (US stocks, US real estate, US bonds in some cases) carry estate tax exposure even for non-US-persons at 40% above US$60k.
- **PFIC trap** — non-US-domiciled mutual funds held by US persons trigger punitive US tax; rules are so bad US tax lawyers generally recommend US-domiciled funds only for US persons.
- **PPLI with US-person beneficiaries** — specifically designed to work within US rules; see [`../products/insurance_ppli.md`](../products/insurance_ppli.md).

### Common US-reach structures

- **Hold US-situs assets via non-US corporate wrapper** — BVI / Cayman company owns the US stocks; individual owns shares of non-US company (which are not US-situs for estate tax).
- **US-compliant PPLI** for US-person beneficiaries.
- **Irrevocable trust structures** with careful US-tax grantor / non-grantor classification.

### When you need US tax counsel (always)

Any time a client's situation involves:
- US citizenship or green card of any family member
- US-situs assets over US$60k (so almost any) in a non-US person's estate
- Trusts with US-person beneficiaries
- US income (rentals, US-dividends, US-based employment)
- Renunciation planning

Do NOT freelance US tax advice. Refer to specialist counsel. Document the referral.

## United Kingdom

### Residency / domicile (pre-2025 regime; changing)

The UK has a uniquely complex system combining **residence** and **domicile**:

- **Residence** — determined by the Statutory Residence Test (days, ties, employment).
- **Domicile** — broader concept based on where the person has their permanent home / intention; inherited from father historically.

### Scope — traditional non-domicile regime (historic)

Historically, **non-domiciled residents** could elect to be taxed on the **remittance basis** — pay UK tax only on UK-source income and income remitted to the UK. This was the "non-dom" regime that made the UK attractive to wealthy migrants.

### 2025 regime change

The UK non-dom regime is being substantially reformed. Post-2025:

- **Remittance basis being phased out.**
- Residence-based taxation replacing domicile-based.
- Transitional arrangements for current non-doms.
- Inheritance tax becoming residence-based (previously domicile-based).

**This is a major change and evolving.** For PB clients with UK connections, specialist UK tax counsel is critical as the new regime takes effect.

### Key UK tax properties

| Tax type | UK rate / treatment |
|---|---|
| **Personal income tax** | Up to 45% |
| **Capital gains tax** | Up to 24% (reformed 2024) |
| **Inheritance tax (IHT)** | **40%** above nil-rate band (~£325k-500k depending on structure) |
| **Stamp duty on property** | Up to 15% surcharge for non-resident buyers |

### Implications for PB clients with UK exposure

- **Children studying / working in UK** — may become UK-resident; tax implications.
- **UK property purchase** — subject to UK SDLT, IHT exposure on UK-situs property.
- **Post-2025 non-dom phase-out** — creates specific planning windows.

## Double-tax treaties and FATCA / CRS overlay

### Double-tax treaties (DTTs)

Bilateral agreements between jurisdictions to:

- Prevent double taxation of same income.
- Reduce withholding-tax rates on cross-border flows (dividends, interest, royalties).
- Provide "tiebreaker" rules when a person is tax-resident in both jurisdictions.

**Key DTTs for Asian PB clients:**

- **SG–US** — limits some withholding on US dividends to 15%.
- **SG–China** — significant for SG-China business flows.
- **HK–mainland China** — CEPA and DTA arrangements.
- **TW** has fewer DTTs than SG / HK — one reason TW-resident wealth faces more friction.

### FATCA / CRS reporting (orthogonal to tax liability)

- FATCA (US-origin) and CRS (OECD multilateral) report **account information to tax authorities** — they don't themselves create new tax.
- But they create **transparency** that drives tax compliance; hidden offshore accounts are much harder post-CRS.
- See [`../regulation/fatca_crs.md`](../regulation/fatca_crs.md) for the full regime.

## Common PB structuring moves (and the tax logic)

| Structure | Primary tax purpose |
|---|---|
| **Hold US stocks via BVI company** | Remove US-situs exposure for non-US-person's estate (40% estate tax avoided) |
| **Irrevocable trust** (offshore domicile) | Separate assets from settlor for estate-tax, asset-protection; jurisdiction-specific |
| **PPLI wrapper** | Tax-deferral on investment growth; tax-free death benefit |
| **SG 13O / 13U family office** | Tax exemption on specified income for SG family-office structures |
| **HK holdco for China investments** | Use of HK-China DTA; capital gains planning |
| **Lifetime gifting (for TW residents)** | Exhaust annual TWD 2.44M exclusion; reduce estate at 20% tax |
| **VCC fund structure (SG)** | Tax-efficient SG vehicle for pooled investments |

## When to bring in a tax specialist (almost always)

Any meaningful structuring decision should involve qualified tax counsel for the relevant jurisdictions. The bank's internal counsel typically can't provide binding tax advice. Specific triggers:

- Cross-border inheritance planning
- Trust set-up or variation
- PPLI structuring
- Any US-person in the family
- Residency change for the client
- Business-sale / liquidity event with tax timing implications
- Inter-generational transfer strategies
- Property purchase across jurisdictions
- Renunciation of any citizenship

Your role: recognise the trigger, frame the issue for the specialist, maintain client relationship through the process. Do NOT freelance tax opinions.

## Interview questions

### Q1. "A TW-resident client has US$10M of US equities. What tax issues should they know?"

**Answer.** Several layers. First, on the TW side — TW residents are taxed on worldwide income, so dividends and capital gains on US equities feed into their TW tax return under the overseas-income minimum-tax regime (above TWD 1M/yr threshold). Second, on the US side — US equity dividends paid to a non-US person are subject to US withholding tax, typically 30% unless reduced by treaty; TW doesn't have a DTA with the US, so no treaty reduction, full 30% withholding. Third — and this is the one often missed — US-situs assets in a non-US person's estate are subject to US estate tax at up to 40% above a US$60k exemption. So a US$10M holding in US stocks held personally triggers approximately US$4M in US estate tax on the client's death. Mitigations I'd discuss with the client: one, hold the US stocks through a BVI or similar non-US corporate wrapper — the shares of the non-US company are not US-situs, so the estate-tax exposure goes away; this adds corporate compliance cost but often justifies at this scale. Two, consider restructuring into non-US-domiciled equity funds (UCITS) that track the same market; reduces both the withholding-tax drag and the estate-tax exposure. Three, insurance-wrapper (PPLI) to convert to tax-efficient transfer. The right mix depends on his overall picture, so I'd propose a session with our SG and TW tax counsel before any structural move.

### Q2. "Why does Singapore work well for UHNW family offices?"

**Answer.** Four structural features, in combination. One, tax regime — SG taxes on territorial basis, so offshore investment income received by SG-resident individuals is generally not taxed. Combined with no CGT and no estate tax, this is already more favourable than most peer jurisdictions. Two, MAS 13O and 13U tax-incentive schemes — qualifying family-office structures receive tax exemption on specified income from designated investments, provided local-hiring and local-spending thresholds are met. This was specifically designed to attract FO relocation and has been the structural driver of the 2020-onwards FO inflow from HK and elsewhere. Three, the VCC — Variable Capital Company corporate structure — provides a tax-efficient vehicle for pooled family investments. Four, political stability and rule of law — SG offers regulatory quality and predictable legal framework that matters to multi-generational planning. For a UHNW family weighing SG vs HK vs Swiss vs Dubai, the 13O / 13U regime is often decisive. Caveats: tighter admission criteria post-2022, quality-of-applicant controls have raised the bar.

### Q3. "A client's adult child just became a US green-card holder. What tax changes?"

**Answer.** Substantial. From the date of green-card issuance, the child is a US person for tax purposes and subject to US worldwide income taxation, FBAR, and FATCA reporting. Three cascading implications. One, the child personally — ongoing US tax filing obligation on all worldwide income, regardless of US residence. If they hold non-US mutual funds or non-US investment companies, they may be exposed to PFIC rules which are punitively taxed. Specialist US tax counsel is essential; they'll need to either restructure holdings into US-compliant form (US-domiciled funds, individual stocks, US-registered products) or accept the PFIC compliance burden. Two, the family trust structure — if the child is a beneficiary of an offshore family trust, the trust now has FATCA reporting obligations relating to them, and the child faces potentially painful "throwback rule" taxation on accumulated trust income distributed to them. The family may want to bifurcate the trust — one part for non-US beneficiaries, one specifically structured for the US-person child. Three, estate planning — the child's US-person status affects gifts to them and inheritances by them. PPLI structures can be particularly valuable here — a US-compliant PPLI wrapper can legitimately convert taxable investment income into tax-free death benefit for the US-person heir. This is the scenario PPLI was specifically designed for. My immediate action: flag to the client within days of learning about the green card, connect them with qualified US tax counsel, document the trigger in the suitability file, and begin the structural review that will take 12–18 months to implement properly.

### Q4. "Compare SG and HK tax environments for a new UHNW client choosing between booking centres."

**Answer.** Structurally very similar at the client level — both are territorial-tax, no CGT, no estate tax, strong treaty networks, developed legal systems. The similarities are more important than the differences for most UHNW individual clients; you could serve the same client well in either jurisdiction from a pure-tax perspective. Where they differ meaningfully: one, family-office regime — SG has the MAS 13O / 13U tax-incentive framework that HK doesn't match. For clients considering building a dedicated FO structure, SG wins on explicit tax incentive. Two, political trajectory — since 2019, HK has faced outflow; SG has received the inflow. Perceived stability over multi-decade horizons is material for multi-generational planning. Three, mainland China access — HK has long been the gateway; CEPA, specific DTAs, easier cross-border flow. If the client's wealth is mainland-China-anchored, HK structural advantages matter. Four, language and cultural proximity — for Mandarin-speaking clients from mainland China, HK is still more natural on daily-servicing; SG works better for ASEAN and broader Asian networks. Five, cost of living and operational cost — both high; SG somewhat higher in recent years. My advice to the client: if the choice is binary, default to SG for new clients today given the stability and incentive regime, with HK as a complementary booking centre for China-specific activity. Ideally hold accounts in both as most major PBs have SG and HK operations; the client can optimise by activity type.

### Q5. "What's the most common tax mistake Asian UHNW clients make with US connections?"

**Answer.** Holding US-situs assets in personal name without understanding the estate-tax exposure. The typical case: an Asian UHNW client builds a large US-equity portfolio over decades — Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, US REITs — and holds them in personal name at a major PB. They've thought about US dividend withholding (and paid it). They've not thought about US estate tax, which hits at 40% above a US$60k exemption on any US-situs assets in a non-US person's estate. Clients are often genuinely surprised to learn their US$20M US-equity book has a US$8M estate-tax exposure on death that their family would have to pay before the assets transfer. Mitigation is often straightforward — wrap the US-situs exposure in a non-US corporate holding vehicle or restructure to non-US-domiciled equivalents (UCITS funds tracking the S&P 500, for example) — but requires someone to raise the issue. My practice when onboarding any client with US equities over US$2–3M is to explicitly raise this: "have you discussed the US estate-tax implications of holding these in personal name? Here's roughly what it could mean; let's bring in US tax counsel to review your structure." The client may or may not act, but the conversation is documented and they've been informed. Failing to raise it is an industry-common lapse that I don't want to repeat.
