# Client Lifecycle — From Prospect to Long-Term Relationship

> **A high-frequency interview question sounds like:** "Walk me through how you'd onboard a new UHNW client, from first call to the first trade, and what the first 90 days look like." This page is the full playbook for that answer — prospecting, first meeting, onboarding paperwork, investment policy statement, first 30/60/90 days, ongoing cadence, annual review, trigger events, and eventual transition or exit. It pulls together KYC, suitability, IPS, and mandate choice into a single temporal flow.

## 直覺理解

客戶生命週期 (client lifecycle) 是 **PB 關係的時間軸**——從完全不認識的 prospect，到簽下 mandate，到第一年把帳戶穩定起來，到每年 review，到多年後的下一代接班或關係結束。每個階段的重點不同：初期是**信任建立**（能不能開始合作），中期是**服務執行**（帳戶穩定、portfolio 執行 IPS），後期是**深化與擴張**（第二帳戶、家人納入、下一代納入）。**出錯最多的是前 30 天**——onboarding 流程慢、首次投資選錯產品、溝通斷層，都會讓一個本來會長成 10 年的關係斷在第 3 個月。

---

## The full lifecycle map

```
PROSPECTING → FIRST MEETING → ONBOARDING → IPS → FIRST 90 DAYS
     ↓              ↓              ↓         ↓         ↓
 Month -6      Month 0       Month 0-1   Month 1   Month 1-3

→ ONGOING SERVICING → ANNUAL REVIEW → DEEPENING → SUCCESSION
        ↓                 ↓              ↓           ↓
    Month 3-12         Year 1         Year 2-5    Year 5+
```

Interviewers probe different stages depending on role:
- RM interviews focus on **prospecting → first meeting → first 90 days → deepening**
- IC interviews focus on **IPS construction → ongoing portfolio decisions → market-event triggers**
- Both need to speak fluently to onboarding mechanics.

## Phase 1 — Prospecting (pre-meeting)

### Source types

| Source | Conversion rate | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Existing-client referral** | High (30–50%) | Fast | Warmest intro; referrer's reputation on the line |
| **Centre-of-influence (CoI)** referral (lawyer, accountant, M&A advisor) | Medium (15–25%) | Medium | Professional context; prospect expects to vet you |
| **Bank-originated lead** (internal leads, marketing response) | Low-medium (5–15%) | Variable | Quality varies hugely by source |
| **Cold network outreach** (alumni, industry conferences, direct) | Low (2–10%) | Slow | Requires pattern of touches before traction |
| **Inbound (client moves to bank proactively)** | Very high (50%+) | Variable | Usually trigger event (liquidity, dissatisfaction with current bank) |

### The prospect qualification checklist

Before investing time in a prospect, run a basic filter:

1. **AUM fit** — is the prospect likely above the bank's minimum (HNW ~US$1M, UHNW ~US$10M, FO-tier ~US$100M)?
2. **Jurisdictional fit** — can your bank book the client? Any sanctions / AML red flags?
3. **Trigger event** — is there a reason they'd move NOW (liquidity event, dissatisfaction, geographic move)?
4. **Fit to platform** — does the bank actually serve their needs (cross-border corridor, specific product, credit appetite)?
5. **Relationship pathway** — is there a warm intro, or does this need to be cold-started?

### Pre-meeting prep

For UHNW prospects, spend 60–90 minutes on research:

- Public corporate filings (if business owner / exec); news coverage.
- LinkedIn / professional network; alumni connections.
- If client is currently at another bank, informal intel on what's working / not.
- Any family / cross-border / succession signals from public information.
- Prepare 2–3 specific conversation hooks drawn from the research (not surveillance-creepy — demonstrate you've done homework).

## Phase 2 — First meeting

**Duration:** 45–75 minutes. Any shorter, you haven't let the client speak; any longer, you've overfilled.

### The first-meeting architecture

| Block | Time | What happens | What the client experiences |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm-up** | 5–10 min | Rapport, introductions, context-setting, clear agenda | "This person is professional and prepared" |
| **Client discovery** (mostly listening) | 20–30 min | Life situation, family, career, goals, concerns, prior banking experience | "This person is actually listening, not pitching" |
| **Your story** (brief) | 5–10 min | Who you are, what your bank does differently, one credential / perspective that fits their situation | "I'd trust this person with my money" |
| **Forward-look / next steps** | 5–10 min | Specific next meeting plan; one simple deliverable you'll send afterwards; no close today | "This is a deliberate person, not rushing" |

### The client-discovery conversation — what to ask

Don't hand them a questionnaire on day one. This is a conversation. Seed topics:

- **Life situation:** family structure, where family members live, health / life-stage considerations, expected next 5 years.
- **Career / wealth origin:** how they built wealth; current operating business or investments.
- **Current banking / advisors:** what works, what doesn't, what's missing.
- **Goals (ranked):** lifestyle preservation; income; wealth growth; wealth transfer; philanthropy; liquidity events; tax efficiency.
- **Concerns:** what keeps them awake; recent market events that mattered to them.
- **Risk signals (not questionnaire yet):** what did they do in 2020? In 2022? How did they experience it?

### Reading the room

- If the prospect asks product / portfolio questions too early — they're testing your depth.
- If the prospect asks about fees / referrals / cross-selling — they're testing your transparency.
- If the prospect asks about their current bank's weaknesses — be neutral; never trash competitors.
- If the prospect goes quiet after personal questions — back off; return to business topics.

### What NOT to do on first meeting

- **No product pitch.** Even if they ask "what would you recommend?" deflect: "that depends on a lot I haven't heard yet; I'd be doing you a disservice to recommend anything today."
- **No paperwork.** No signature requests, no KYC forms.
- **No close.** "Would you open an account with us?" kills the relationship.
- **No bragging.** "Our bank is #1 in X" — they've heard it. Let the facts speak through conversation.

### Post-meeting follow-up (within 24–48 hours)

Send one thoughtful follow-up: summary note of the conversation, one piece of research relevant to their situation, proposed date for the next meeting. Keep it short (<250 words). This is the first demonstration of reliability.

## Phase 3 — Onboarding (Month 0–1)

Once the prospect agrees to move forward, onboarding is a 2–6 week operational sprint. This is where many deals die of friction.

### The onboarding pipeline

| Step | Duration | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Initial KYC** — identity documents, proof of address, biographical | Days 1–3 | Client + onboarding team | Passport / ID copy, utility bill, etc. |
| **CDD / AML** — beneficial ownership, SOW / SOF, PEP screening | Days 3–10 | RM + compliance | Documented SOW narrative, corroborated |
| **FATCA / CRS self-certification** | Days 3–7 | Client | W-9 or W-8BEN, CRS form |
| **AI / EI opt-in** (if eligible) | Days 5–10 | Client + RM | Threshold evidence + signed opt-in |
| **Sanctions screening** | Days 3–5 | Compliance | Clearance memo |
| **Account opening** (custody, cash, currency sub-accounts) | Days 7–14 | Operations | Account numbers live |
| **Internal approvals** — senior management sign-off for EDD / higher-risk clients | Days 10–21 | Senior RM + compliance | Memo sign-off |
| **Funding** — client wires / transfers initial funds | Days 14–21 | Client + ops | Funds in custody |
| **Mandate documentation** — DPM agreement, advisory agreement, Lombard facility if applicable | Days 14–28 | RM + legal + client | Signed agreements |
| **First investment meeting** — IPS + initial allocation | Days 21–35 | RM + IC + client | Signed IPS + first trades queued |

**High-risk clients (PEP, EDD cases):** add 2–4 weeks for escalated approvals.

### The anti-friction principles

UHNW prospects are allergic to friction. Three rules:

1. **Consolidate document asks.** Don't email "one more thing" 14 times; bundle requests into 2–3 rounds.
2. **Translate compliance-speak.** Don't forward "MAS requires Category-5 EDD documentation" language; translate to "we'll need a copy of your tax return and a one-page summary of your family business history."
3. **Weekly status update.** Even if nothing has changed, send a "here's where we are" update. Silence reads as dysfunction.

### Common onboarding failure modes

- **"The bank lost my documents."** Happens. Recover: apologise cleanly, personally escalate, re-request.
- **Client impatience with KYC depth.** Reframe: "The MAS expectations after 1MDB mean every bank asks the same questions; I'd rather be thorough now than have issues later."
- **SOW narrative doesn't match documents.** STOP the onboarding; escalate; never paper over.
- **Referee call from prior bank goes badly.** Usually survivable with a frank conversation with the prospect.
- **FATCA surprise** — prospect discovers US-person status they didn't know about (accidental American, green card). Requires specialist referral; don't try to freelance tax.

## Phase 4 — Investment Policy Statement (IPS)

**The IPS is the governing document of the relationship.** It's what you reference when the client panics in a drawdown and wants to sell everything; it's the audit trail when suitability is challenged; it's the shared reference that says "here's what we both agreed we're doing and why."

### What an IPS contains

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Client profile** | Identity, family, jurisdiction, tax status, AI/EI classification |
| **Wealth picture** | Total net worth, liquid vs illiquid, currency exposure, sources |
| **Objectives** (ranked) | Income need, wealth growth target, wealth transfer, philanthropy, liquidity events |
| **Constraints** | Liquidity needs, horizon, ESG preferences, legal / religious restrictions, concentration limits |
| **Risk tolerance** | Stated (questionnaire), practical (behavioural), triangulated |
| **Strategic Asset Allocation (SAA)** | Target weights across asset classes with ranges |
| **Tactical Asset Allocation (TAA) framework** | How and by whom tilts are made |
| **Mandate structure** | DPM / Advisory / hybrid split |
| **Rebalancing policy** | Calendar, threshold, or hybrid |
| **Review cadence** | Quarterly, semi-annual, annual |
| **Governance** | Who has signing authority, decision process for large trades |
| **Success metrics** | Return benchmark, risk ceiling, drawdown tolerance |
| **Review triggers** | Events that force an interim review (liquidity, major market event, life change) |

### IPS meeting (the second investment meeting)

- Present a draft IPS; walk through every section.
- Encourage pushback: "what doesn't feel right?"
- Sign only after the client has read it, understood it, and can repeat the core commitments.
- Refresh annually; update on material life changes.

**An IPS that was never discussed or that sits in a drawer is useless.** The value is in the shared understanding, not the document.

## Phase 5 — First 90 days

The first 90 days determine whether this relationship builds trust or bleeds it. Treat this as a structured project.

### Day 0 — funding arrives

- Confirm receipt, send welcome note, brief on initial liquidity plan.
- Park the funds in T-bills / money market while IPS is finalised. Do not rush into investment.

### Days 1–30 — initial deployment

- IPS signed by Day 14.
- Core SAA execution: bond ladder started, equity ETFs positioned, REIT / alt commitments queued.
- Weekly touchpoint — brief call or email — for the first 4 weeks.
- Document every trade with a suitability memo (especially the first 3 months — audit trail is forming).

### Days 30–60 — deepening

- First monthly statement review with the client. Walk through: what was bought, why, how it's doing, any surprises.
- Introduce extended team: backup RM, IC, product specialist. "Here's who covers you when I'm away."
- Identify any gaps from initial discovery: missing family members in account structure, adult children's needs, philanthropy intent.

### Days 60–90 — transition to steady-state

- First "quarterly-style" review (early cadence): portfolio summary, any TAA tilts, house view update, client feedback.
- Shift to monthly touchpoints going forward (or agreed cadence).
- Begin referral-chain cultivation (lightweight; see [`../bq/stories/09_cold_outreach_win.md`](../bq/stories/09_cold_outreach_win.md) for approach).

### What interviewers probe

- "What if the client wants to go all-in on a specific trade at Day 15?" → Pause. Refer back to IPS draft; explain it's outside the agreed risk profile; propose an alternative structure (satellite sleeve, smaller size) or defer.
- "What if the first trade goes south in Week 2?" → Face into it. Schedule the client call immediately; frame in IPS context; reference the drawdown-tolerance band; don't pretend the MTM isn't negative.
- "What if the client's spouse pushes back on the plan at Day 30?" → Take it seriously; schedule a joint meeting; integrate the spouse's view; adjust IPS if needed.

## Phase 6 — Ongoing servicing (Months 3–12)

### Standard cadence

| Rhythm | Activity |
|---|---|
| Daily | Intraday monitoring of key holdings, Lombard margin, any alerts |
| Weekly | Brief market update to client; 1–2 touchpoints per month typical for most clients |
| Monthly | Statement review, performance summary; some clients want a call, others email |
| Quarterly | Formal review: IPS compliance, performance vs benchmark, TAA update, rebalancing decisions |
| Semi-annual | CIO house view refresh, broader strategic conversation |
| Annual | Full IPS review, suitability refresh, AI re-opt-in, FATCA / CRS re-certification if triggered, family / life-event check-in |

### Documentation discipline

- Every trade: suitability memo with client context + rationale. Retain 5+ years.
- Every material conversation: note in file with date, topic, decisions.
- Every drift from IPS: documented with rationale (e.g. tactical satellite position outside SAA band, flagged and client-acknowledged).

This is both good service AND suitability defence — if MAS or internal audit reviews your book, the file quality is what matters.

---

## Phase 7 — Annual review

### What to cover

1. **Portfolio performance** — absolute and vs benchmark; attribute returns.
2. **IPS compliance** — did we stay within ranges? Exceptions?
3. **Suitability refresh** — any life events? Risk tolerance changes? Updated KYC.
4. **SAA re-evaluation** — is the allocation still right for the horizon and goals?
5. **Product shelf updates** — new capabilities, manager changes, fee changes.
6. **Market / macro view** — how have last 12 months shifted your house view?
7. **Forward-look** — themes for next 12 months; tactical priorities.
8. **Administrative** — AI opt-in renewal, FATCA / CRS re-cert if residency changed, any new family members to add to account structure.

### What the best RMs add

- **Proactive ideas** — "given your son's graduation and likely move to UK, here's what we should think about."
- **Non-portfolio value** — introductions to relevant professionals (estate lawyer, tax specialist), educational content for next-gen family members.
- **Strategic conversations** — "you're reaching a life-stage where succession / philanthropy / family governance deserves dedicated attention; shall we plan a separate working session?"

## Phase 8 — Deepening and expansion (Year 2–5)

### Signals the relationship is ready to deepen

- Client volunteers information beyond their PB account (business decisions, family dynamics, next-gen introductions).
- Client proactively refers network.
- Client consolidates assets from other PBs toward yours.
- Client engages on broader topics: succession, philanthropy, family office.

### Deepening moves

- **Adult children onboarding:** separate accounts for each, build primary relationship with next-gen.
- **Spouse fully integrated:** joint reviews, spouse-led philanthropy sleeve.
- **Extended family network:** parents, siblings, in-laws — but only where trust cascade naturally, never pushed.
- **Structural upgrades:** from individual to family trust; from simple DPM to DPM + advisory + alt commitments; from SG-only to multi-jurisdictional booking.
- **Family-office conversation** if AUM > US$100M: would SFO / MFO structure fit? See [`estate_planning.md`](estate_planning.md).

## Phase 9 — Trigger events during the relationship

Events that force unscheduled reviews and actions:

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| **Major market event** (crisis, writedown, dislocation) | Emergency book triage; high-exposure clients get same-day calls. See [`../bq/stories/10_regulatory_change_adapt.md`](../bq/stories/10_regulatory_change_adapt.md). |
| **Client life event** (illness, death in family, divorce, new child, retirement) | Pause investment activity as appropriate; re-run IPS; update legal structures. |
| **Liquidity event** (business sale, IPO, inheritance) | See [`../cases/04_liquidity_event.md`](../cases/04_liquidity_event.md); staged deployment discipline. |
| **Jurisdictional change** (residency move, citizenship change, green card) | Tax and FATCA / CRS re-assessment; may restructure accounts. |
| **Regulatory change** (new MAS notice, new tax treaty, sanctions expansion) | Book-wide review of affected positions; proactive client outreach. |
| **Bank-side change** (RM departure, team restructure, bank merger) | Continuity plan; warm handoff; maintain relationship quality. |

## Phase 10 — Exit, transfer, or succession

Relationships end. Handle them well — how you handle an ending is often what past clients remember.

### Client-initiated exit

- Thank them for the relationship (no bitterness).
- Ask specifically why — learn.
- Don't counter-pitch aggressively; if the decision is made, accept it.
- Execute the transfer cleanly: prompt response to new bank's document requests, clean handover of custody and open positions.
- Maintain light professional contact; the door stays open. See [`../bq/stories/06_lost_client_recovery.md`](../bq/stories/06_lost_client_recovery.md).

### Your exit from the bank

- Non-solicitation clauses apply (usually 6–24 months). Do not actively solicit former clients during this period.
- Hand over the book cleanly to a successor; complete file notes; ensure continuity for each client.
- Post non-solicit, clients may reach out; be professional about re-engagement.

### Client succession (death of primary account holder)

- Immediate: probate process; freeze certain activities; maintain support for surviving spouse / executors.
- Medium-term: successor-trustee arrangements activate; next-generation accounts formalise.
- Long-term: multi-generational relationship continuity — the best RMs have sat with 3 generations of one family.

## Interview questions you should be able to answer

### Q1. "Walk me through onboarding a new UHNW client — first call to first trade."

**Answer.** I'd structure it as: first call, first meeting, onboarding, IPS, first trade. First call — 15 minutes, qualify fit, schedule the first meeting with an agenda sent in advance. First meeting — 60 minutes face-to-face if possible, roughly 10 minutes of rapport and context, 30 minutes of client discovery listening, 10 minutes of my story, 10 minutes of forward-look. No product pitch, no signing. I'd follow up within 48 hours with a thoughtful summary. Onboarding kicks off next — I'd bundle the KYC / CDD / FATCA / AI opt-in document requests into 2–3 rounds rather than spreading them out; compliance team runs in parallel on sanctions and adverse-media screening. Weekly status update regardless of change. Target: signed mandate documentation in 4–5 weeks. Between mandate signing and first trade, we'd have the IPS meeting — probably meeting 3 or 4 — where we walk through a draft IPS in detail, get pushback, sign only after the client can articulate it back to me. First trade follows IPS signature, starting with low-risk bond-ladder construction rather than aggressive first moves. Weekly touchpoints through the first month; daily monitoring from Day 1. I'd aim for the first month to feel over-serviced rather than under — it's cheaper to build trust now than to rebuild it after a bad start.

### Q2. "What's the biggest failure mode in the first 90 days?"

**Answer.** Two, roughly tied. First — onboarding friction kills more PB relationships than anything else. The client has moved US$20M to your bank; they expect to be investing by Week 3; they're still chasing documentation by Week 6. The feeling becomes "this bank is dysfunctional" and they tell their network. The fix is operational: bundle document asks, translate compliance-speak, never let a week pass without a status update from me personally. Second — over-eager initial deployment. Client wants to be "invested"; bank has AUM targets; the temptation is to execute the full SAA in month 1. Then a Week-3 market drawdown hits fresh positions and the client panics before any relationship equity has built. The fix is discipline: park in T-bills / money-market while IPS is being drafted; execute the bond ladder first (slowest to drawdown); phase equity deployment over 2–3 months; don't touch alts or structured products until month 2–3. The whole first quarter is about building capability to survive the eventual drawdown, not maximising early return.

### Q3. "Client calls you at 7 AM saying the market has crashed and they want to sell everything. How do you respond?"

**Answer.** Don't answer by phone unless they demand it. Request a same-day meeting — face-to-face or video. Use the time between call and meeting to pull the IPS we agreed and the drawdown scenarios we documented. In the meeting: listen first, don't pitch. Let them and their spouse if present walk through the fear fully. Then — without being lecturing — re-ground in the IPS: we anticipated a drawdown of this magnitude, here's the agreed plan, selling at this point crystallises losses. Run the math with them: what happens if we sell now versus hold 6 months — historical base rates for comparable episodes. Offer one small action they can take to reduce anxiety — say 5% of equity moved to T-bills — not to derail the long-term plan but to give them psychological buffer. Schedule daily 5-minute check-ins for the next 10 business days. The goal isn't to "win the argument" — it's to hold the IPS discipline while genuinely hearing and validating the fear. See [`../bq/stories/03_market_volatility_client.md`](../bq/stories/03_market_volatility_client.md) for the fuller story.

### Q4. "How do you know when a client relationship is ready to deepen — to add family members, to upgrade to DPM, to take on alternatives?"

**Answer.** Three signals. First — the client volunteers information beyond their PB account. They start telling you about their business decisions, family tensions, the sale they're considering. This means they're treating you as a trusted advisor, not a product vendor. Second — they proactively refer network. A referral with their reputation attached is the single clearest signal of durable trust. Third — they consolidate. Moving assets to you from other banks. They wouldn't do that if they weren't high-conviction. When you see two of those three, the relationship can bear expansion — more products, family onboarding, bigger commitments. Before those signals, pushing expansion is premature and reads as sales-driven; after those signals, you can have the strategic conversation about family governance, succession, or a family office move. The best RMs let the client signal the readiness; they don't force it.

### Q5. "What's the right cadence of client contact after the first 90 days?"

**Answer.** Depends on segment, market conditions, and client preference — but a durable default is: monthly touchpoint (short note, one market insight or portfolio observation), quarterly formal review (IPS compliance + performance), semi-annual strategic conversation (house view, tactical), annual full review. In stress periods — market events, life events — tighten to weekly or daily. In very stable periods with a low-touch client, can loosen slightly but never go dark for more than 6 weeks. Key discipline: calendar the contact in advance and treat it as a commitment, not a "I'll email them when I have something." Silence is how relationships decay.
